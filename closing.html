Closing report: the satellite and flight chapter closed 6 Oct 2026

This closes the satcom and flight-model branch of the investigation. The drift branch remains open and is not covered here. Written against the corrected record, after TASK_026 overturned two things this chapter previously asserted.

No part of this investigation located the aircraft. No source in the archive produces a final coordinate, and nothing in TASK_021, TASK_022, TASK_026 or TASK_029 produces one either. Every position in this atlas is a conditional model state. That is the result, stated first rather than qualified at the end.

What was established

1. The cruise model fits, under heavy constraint

C120 fits the nine-term score at χ² = 8.117071 with nine active constraints, including k pinned at its lower bound, start latitude pinned at its 0.5° limit, and a fuel allowance margin of about 0.1 kg. It is a fuel-limited solution, not a free optimum. Within the TASK_021 diagnostic it does not require a slow startup: forcing one costs +0.62 in χ². The C-box variant failed to converge; its flight verified independently but it cannot separate the bound widening from extra optimisation of a non-stationary reference.

2. The endpoint latitude is constrained by the handshake sequence — within the region the model can see

Seven solutions in this family land at 00:11 between 35.85°S and 36.85°S. TASK_029 found that northern commands reach 23.9°S to 26.4°S with fuel to spare, but miss every handshake ring by 108 to 127 km, at 24.8 to 29.2σ. Those northern flights are also still powered at 00:25, so they cannot produce the 00:19 log-on.

Updated 10 October — the northern question has now been asked and answered, provisionally. This finding was first stated as a physical exclusion of northern endpoints, then corrected on 7 October when a coarse sweep indicated the opposite, then flagged as untestable because the forcing data stopped at 100°E. The forcing was extended and the question is now live.

There is a northern candidate at 32.00°S, 95.26°E scoring χ² = 16.84, found from two bins converging on the same point from opposite directions. It is not a coarse-grid artifact. It scores worse than the southern solution at 7.92, and the entire difference — 8.93 — is linear in the frequency bias at 1.568 per hertz, so a common offset of +5.69 Hz would make the two indistinguishable.

The bias has since been measured against the published communication logs, and it points the other way: −1.39 Hz pooled, −0.36 Hz on the channel the scored events use. Applying it widens the gap to between 9.50 and 11.10, and at the pooled figure the northern candidate scores 20.91 and fails the χ² ≤ 20 gate it was admitted under. What remains unmeasured is how far the bias drifts in the four hours between that calibration and the scored window. The full state of the comparison is on the north–south page.

3. Twelve terminal continuations match the final frequency pair, and none is a location

TASK_022 searched 582 histories from C120’s frozen engine-loss state. Twelve matched the adopted envelopes. All twelve arrive at the water supersonic, at 2.1 to 2.7 times Vmo, banked 76° to 89°, inside the aerodynamic proxy the model itself flags as unsupported. An aircraft in that descent breaks up well above the surface, so debris would not reach those coordinates. The two controls that stay inside the supported aerodynamic domain and arrive at survivable speed miss the 00:19:37 BFO by more than 100 Hz.

Superseded 10 October — the last sentence is not a general truth. It held for TASK_022’s two controls under C120. Under a different parent it fails: TASK_039 produced a continuation that arrives at 1.1 times Vmo and fits the final frequency pair to +0.82 and −0.42 Hz, well inside 1σ.

The final frequency pair does not discriminate arrival speed. S11 and S16 are the same law with the same drag increment, the same bank angle, the same onset and identical residuals — S16 is S11 with the bank released. The release happens after the last transmission, so the observations cannot see it, and the two arrive 200 m/s apart.

4. The compensation convention is published

Holland (arXiv 1702.02432v2), sections IV-A and IV-B, describes the SDU calculation as using ground speed and track, zero vertical speed, nominal satellite position and sea-level aircraft position, with the physical Doppler term separately carrying actual vertical motion. That is the convention in use. The “K-mode OPEN” label concerned historical callers that forced physical up velocity to zero, not this convention.

5. The BTO residuals were never a miss

TASK_022’s −24.98 to −26.69 µs residuals are 0.86 to 0.92σ against the project’s own listed σBTO of 29.0 µs. The 1 µs target was 0.03σ, about thirty times tighter than the measurement, and was declared as a numerical search target rather than an acceptance rule. The residual spans only 1.71 µs across all twelve, so it is inherited from the frozen 00:11 state and the terminal law cannot move it.

What was tested and did not hold

  • The terminal conclusion, as originally stated. “The final BFO pair requires a steep descent and excludes a long controlled glide” was never established by TASK_022 and does not survive TASK_026. The defensible statement is narrower: those twelve selected imposed-force histories matched the envelopes at the published operator, and those two specific controls did not.
  • The frequency matches are fragile. Sweeping the vertical-compensation fraction κ, all twelve hold to κ = 0.042, the last warm-up case ends at κ = 0.269, and nothing survives past κ = 0.317.
  • But there is no inversion. R0 and G0 never become compatible anywhere in [0, 1], under any geometry switch. The sensitivity runs from “dives match” to “nothing matches”, never to “glides match”.
  • The cruise score cannot arbitrate. χ² moves 0.00255 across the whole κ range, with a largest BFO change of 0.116 Hz against σBFO of 4.3. Measurably non-flat, and far too small to discriminate.
  • Northern arc-1 starts look plausible and cannot be tested. Under a grid 145× coarser than the known solution requires, starts at 4–6°N score ten times better than C120's own start, with Mach pinned at the grid ceiling. Whether that survives proper resolution is unknown and currently unknowable.
  • The arc north of 27°S is outside the model and outside the search. The northern limit of every seabed search box in the project manifest is 27°S; the arc leaves the forcing data at 27.5°S. About 1,460 km of arc, from 27°S to 15°S, falls outside both. The ERA5 subset was evidently clipped to the region the search already assumed, so the model has never been able to challenge the assumption that defined its inputs.
  • A one-engine fuel hypothesis was retired. The README warns that single-engine burn may be too generous, which would add range and push the endpoint south. The evaluator has no single-engine phase, so the warning does not apply to it.

Three things that stop this being a result.
  • Being inside an outline is not having been searched. “Searched and not found” constrains an endpoint only where the probability of detection was high, and an archived coverage outline establishes nothing about that. This caveat is the execution agent’s own and it is the most important line in the package.
  • The membership margins are smaller than a convention choice. These distances are measured against the zero-height 18,400 µs locus. The published arc this atlas draws is for an aircraft at 40,000 ft, and for the same timing value the two differ by 9.8 km on the ground — larger than the 3.68 km that puts S16 inside Deep Tow and the 3.58 km that puts a C120 contact outside it. Zero height is the defensible choice for a surface contact; the point is that the margin is not resolving anything.
  • Only the primary rows are evidence. Where a primary missed, a secondary was fitted with two parameters against two observations. It is exactly determined, so its χ² is identically zero by construction, for every case. Those rows are interpolations, not fits. Four of fourteen primaries have both residuals inside 2σ, and S11/S16 is the only one inside 1σ.
What this family cannot contain: a long glide. Every law in the run is a steeply banked descent, 76° to 89°. A banked descent spirals, so a long duration is not a long distance — these continuations run 2.5 to 14.1 minutes from engine loss and still reach the surface only 18 to 61 km beyond the arc. The shallowest arrival in the set descends at 8.8 m/s against 123 m/s forward, an instantaneous ratio of 14 to 1, which is glide-like in gradient but is still flying a circle.

A wings-level glide from cruise altitude at 15:1 covers about 180 km of ground track, and at 20:1 about 240 km. Nothing in this search space reaches a third of that. No glide is in TASK_039’s search space — not as a result, but by the shape of the laws being swept. Glide-adjacent controls were run once, under C120, and missed the final frequency value by more than 100 Hz; that result is superseded above as a general claim and has not been repeated under the current parent.

Scope of that statement, 10 October. It covers TASK_039 and what this atlas holds. A longer-running southern case — order 40 minutes and about 170 km of glide, near 38°S — has been recalled from elsewhere in the project. It is not in the TASK_039 package, whose contacts span 36.50°S to 37.59°S and 2.5 to 14.1 minutes, nor in the Phase 5A candidate set, whose entries near that latitude sit 121 to 677 km from the arc and are final-handshake positions rather than terminal contacts. If that case exists, the paragraph above understates what has been tried and should be replaced by its numbers rather than qualified.

Reconstruction, not the record. It is described as an older C120 computation with a terminal event attached, so the geometry can be checked against C120’s own frozen parameters. Taking its recorded 00:11 position of 35.848°S, 90.393°E, flying its recorded cruise bearing of 185.8° at 462 kt to its recorded engine-loss time of 00:17:14, then gliding 170 km on that heading, ends at 38.16°S, 90.10°E — which matches the recalled latitude. The implied glide ratio, 170 km over about 11.7 km of descent, is 14.5 to 1 and unremarkable for the type.

That endpoint would sit 102 km beyond the 7th arc and outside all three search outlines — roughly 1.7 times further out than any TASK_039 contact, in water none of the three searches covered. One element does not reconcile: 170 km in 40 minutes is 71 m/s, about 138 kt of ground speed, which is slow for a gliding 777 and would need either a different reference for the 40 minutes or a headwind. These figures are a reconstruction from C120’s recorded parameters and are not the archived case. They are here to be replaced by it, not to stand in for it.

These coordinates are not locations and must not be propagated as any. They inherit a frozen parent, an unresolved frequency bias, and a terminal law that is imposed rather than inferred. A small residual on two observations is not evidence that the imposed law occurred.

Appendix: readings that were wrong, including mine

  • I called the compensation convention unvalidated. It is published in Holland. The flight page asserted the opposite for a day and has been corrected.
  • I read “K-mode OPEN” as evidence about that convention. It concerned legacy callers forcing physical up velocity to zero. Different thing entirely.
  • I wrote that the final BFO pair requires a steep descent and excludes a long glide. TASK_022 never established that, and I should not have summarised it that way. Withdrawing it did not settle the question either: as of 10 October no long glide has been run in this project, because every terminal law in use is a steep banked spiral and a glide is not a member of that family. The claim was wrong and its replacement is a gap, not an answer.
  • I predicted the cruise χ² would be flat in κ and had a circularity argument prepared. It is measurably non-flat, and Codex correctly declined to draw the conclusion I had anticipated.
  • I specified a half-normal kernel that converges to a 100 km indicator. It does not; it converges to a point mass. The verification step built on that was wrong and was replaced.
  • I fixed the initial bearing and set a Mach floor of 0.70 in the first reachability directive. Either alone would have made a northern endpoint unreachable by construction rather than by physics.
  • I expected fuel to bind the northern reach. The signals bind; fuel has tonnes to spare.
  • I then read TASK_030's table as confirming the south. It does the opposite. I compared absolute χ² across starts without first checking what that grid achieves at the one start where the right answer is known — 1,174 against 8.117. With that in view the 112 at 6°N is the headline, and I had buried it. Ryan caught this.
  • I read the northern BTO residuals as a physical exclusion. They are a forcing-coverage artifact: the arc north of 27.5°S lies outside the weather data, so no flight could reach it regardless of physics. Corrected 7 October.
  • I predicted the dt 15 s mesh would halve the sweep cost. It saved 8%, and I then inferred a fixed overhead from two benchmarks that turned out to use the same timestep, so their difference measured nothing. The execution agent's diagnostic contradicted both.
  • I told the execution agent the density field was unsmoothed. That was true of the locator in common.js, not of the field, which TASK_021 Part A confirmed is smoothed upstream. It matters: smoothing pulls a maximum toward broad mass and so biases toward exactly the verdict I had predicted.
  • I specified a synthetic harvester the archive already recorded as returning zero matches. The strict match rule had already failed for five fixed synthetic sites. A pilot now fails in minutes instead of hours. Both this and the item above were caught by the execution agent’s questions, not by me.
  • I proposed that the northern candidate’s frequency residuals were a mis-modelled station-Doppler term, not a bias. The satellite’s drift away from the ground station sweeps 44.7 Hz across exactly the scored observations, and I had just traced an 86 km timing error to that same term, so it looked like the same mistake twice. The preregistered test was to correlate the residuals against the station’s range rate: it needed correlation near +1 and a null at 19:41. Every record came back negative (−0.88, −0.26, −0.34) and every one peaked at 19:41, where the term vanishes. Refuted, and the offset sweep I had called the wrong instrument was the right one.
  • I read the latitude bins as a trend falling toward the south. The sequence is 538, 187, 36, 2026, 22, 17, 17, 8, 8. The 2,026 sits between neighbours of 36 and 22. Those are search failures, not topography, and the bins were measuring the optimiser as much as the aircraft.
  • I let “all twelve arrive supersonic” harden into a general rule about the terminal family. It was a fact about TASK_022 under C120. A different parent produces arrivals from Mach 0.35 to 1.34, and the best-fitting case of all arrives at 1.1 times Vmo. The frequency pair never constrained arrival speed, because what separates those outcomes happens after the last transmission.
  • Superseded: the stale Task 9 text on the combined map, corrected in atlas v6.

For whoever picks this up

  • The BFO bias calibration epoch is the single most consequential unresolved item in this chapter. Everything frequency-based inherits it.
  • Three histories (S03, S07, S11) need no warm-up offset at all. S03 fits all three final observations within 2σ. If the warm-up envelope is ever tightened on evidence, those three are the ones that survive it.
  • The twelve contact coordinates must not be propagated as locations, into drift work or anywhere else. The two branches were deliberately kept separate and should stay that way.
  • A northern endpoint reachable only by a path with turns, step climbs or a speed schedule would not appear in TASK_029 or TASK_030, which tested only the commanded-flight family as implemented.
  • The long glide is the largest open hypothesis in this chapter. It has never been excluded. Whether it has ever been run is itself unresolved: nothing in the packages this atlas was built from contains one, but a longer southern case near 38°S has been recalled from elsewhere in the project and not yet located. The terminal search is parameterised as banked spirals, so a wings-level glide is outside the family by construction rather than by result — and a glide would reach three to four times further beyond the arc than anything tested, into water no search has covered. Testing it needs a terminal law with bank near zero, not a finer sweep of the laws already in use.
  • The single highest-value next step is data, not computation: extending ERA5 east to 120°E and north to 20°N. Until the forcing covers the arc, the northern question cannot be asked — and the sweep that could not ask it still pointed that way.
