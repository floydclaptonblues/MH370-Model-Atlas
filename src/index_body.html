9M-MRO · Malaysia Airlines MH370 · 7–8 March 2014

The last flight of 9M-MRO, reconstructed from measurement and physics

After the aircraft left radar coverage, its satellite terminal kept exchanging link-maintenance messages with the Inmarsat ground station at Perth, relayed by the Inmarsat-3F1 satellite over the Indian Ocean. Each exchange recorded two numbers. One is the round-trip light-travel time between station, satellite and aircraft (BTO). The other is the frequency offset left by Doppler shift along the same path (BFO). This atlas models what those numbers can and cannot say about where the aircraft went. It does three things:

  • flies a Boeing 777-200ER through the recorded weather;
  • tests each simulated path against the satellite record;
  • carries floating debris through four and a half years of reanalysed ocean currents.
No part of this work locates the aircraft. Every coordinate on these pages is a conditional model state: it holds only under the assumptions stated with it. Results later shown to be wrong are withdrawn in place, dated, struck through and explained, not silently replaced. The full revision history is public on GitHub.

Three physical problems

1 · Satellite geometry measurement model frozen

Measured. Two quantities per exchange.

BTO = 2·(dsat→aircraft + dsat→station)/c + bias
BFO = Δfuplink + Δfdownlink + δfterminal compensation + δfsatellite + δfstation AFC + bias

BTO fixes the aircraft's distance from the satellite, which defines a ring on the Earth's surface. BFO responds to velocity along the line to the satellite. The terminal pre-corrects for its own horizontal motion, so BFO is about 35 times more sensitive to vertical speed than to horizontal speed.

Inputs: the Malaysian release of the SATCOM signalling log (19 handshake rows checked line by line against it, 14 of 14 exact); the published satellite position and velocity table. Calibration: frequency bias measured with the aircraft stationary at the gate, −1.39 Hz pooled over 44 samples.

2 · Aircraft dynamics conditional

Integrated. A point-mass Boeing 777-200ER with Trent 892 engines flies commanded course, Mach number and pressure level. It flies through ERA5 reanalysis wind and temperature on pressure levels. Mass falls as fuel burns.

CL = 2nW / (ρV²S) ≤ CL,buffet,  Trequired ≤ Tavailable,  fuel ≥ 0

Every path is checked at each step against lift, buffet and thrust limits, and against the fuel on board. Its predicted BTO and BFO are then compared with the log. The performance deck reproduces certified Trent 892 engine-out level-off altitudes to within 43 ft at this aircraft's mass.

Limit, stated plainly: nine scored measurements against six free parameters leave three nominal degrees of freedom. A close fit shows that nine numbers cannot refute the family; it does not show that the aircraft flew one course. No fit has yet been shown to be the minimum of its family.

3 · Ocean transport executed

Integrated. Floating objects move with the sea surface plus a fixed fraction of the wind.

dx/dt = uocean(x, t) + 0.012·U10(x, t)

Currents are daily GLORYS12V1 surface fields on a 1/12° mesh (Copernicus Marine). Wind is NCEP/NCAR 10 m. The domain covers 15–130°E and 60°S–15°N, from 8 March 2014 to 31 August 2018. The 1.2% wind slip is fixed and is never adjusted to fit a find.

Result: in year one the objects travel east. 233–256 of 481 cross 80°E westward in year two, and 168 reach 50°E in year three. Halving the timestep moves this by 5 objects. Inverse problem: the source region cannot be determined from the finds, because the answer moves 1,000–1,900 km when an unmeasured assumption changes.

Where the evidence stands

Each row is a physical question, its standing and the evidence in physical units. The chapters below give the full record, including what was withdrawn on the way.

Did the aircraft fly north to about 24–26°S on the 7th arc?rejectedCommanded flights reaching 23.9–26.4°S miss every handshake ring by 108–127 km (24.8–29.2σ). They are still under power at 00:25, so they cannot produce the 00:19 log-on. A margin of this size does not depend on any search having converged.
Northern basin near 32°S, or southern near 36°S?unresolvedWith the optimiser defect corrected, the two scores are 4.840 and 4.821, a difference of 0.02. A full enumeration of the 19:41 state surface finds two deep basins. Which basin is deeper depends on whether the common frequency offset is held fixed or estimated with the fit.
A long controlled glide after fuel exhaustion?rejectedThe glide G0/S14 reaches the water 168 km beyond the 7th arc, wings level at 196 kt. It misses the final frequency pair by +34.2 Hz and −121.4 Hz. No setting of the terminal's vertical-compensation fraction between 0 and 1 makes a glide compatible.
Does the final frequency pair require a supersonic dive?not establishedAcross 72 terminal continuations, arrival speed ranges from Mach 0.35 to 1.34. One continuation arriving at 1.1× the maximum operating speed fits the pair to +0.82 and −0.42 Hz. What separates a survivable arrival from a supersonic one happens after the last transmission.
Could one engine hold the cruise between the two flameouts?partly answeredNo. The commanded Mach 0.831 cannot be held, and the best single-engine Mach is 0.688–0.698, about 17% less true airspeed. Whether the aircraft also had to descend depends on which certified thrust rating applies, and remains open.
Is the cruise a single commanded course?not testable hereThere are three nominal degrees of freedom. For the 22:41 timing value, which is held out of every fit, the observed round-trip time is 43 to 88 µs longer than predicted at every admissible start. In every case the aircraft was farther from the satellite than the model places it. That is about 60% wider than a correct model of this size gives. The diagnostic is real, but its significance is not calibrated.
Where was the aircraft at 19:41?assumedThe published record contains no connected flight from the primary-radar handoff that reaches 19:41. Two reconstructed paths stopped near 18:27 with a thrust shortfall under 1%. The 19:41 state and fuel load are carried from historical context.
Where did the debris come from?not determinableThe method recovers known synthetic sources. On the real finds, its answer moves 1,000–1,900 km when an unmeasured assumption changes, which is further than any single estimate is wide.

How the work is checked

  • Thresholds are fixed before the run. The briefs for every computation are kept in directives/, including superseded versions. Several thresholds were set in advance and then not met; those outcomes were reported as they came out, not adjusted afterwards.
  • No parameter is tuned toward the 7th arc, a flight path or a debris find. The atmosphere, aircraft limits, measurement calibration and wind slip are held fixed across every comparison.
  • Measurements and model outputs are kept apart. The canonical map shows only the observation record and third-party fields, with every model output listed as excluded. The satellite and drift branches share no coordinates.
  • Errors are withdrawn in place. On 22 October the optimiser was found to be receiving constraint derivatives in place of objective derivatives, because of a strided gradient array. The defect was corrected and the campaign re-run. The north–south margin built on the old fits fell from 8.93 to 0.02, and it was withdrawn on the page where it was published. The dated record is in the closing report and CORRECTION_AUDIT_20261023.md.
  • Every tolerance is converted to physical units. The methods page gives each tolerance in metres, metres per second or feet per minute.
