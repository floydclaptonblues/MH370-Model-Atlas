9M-MRO · MALAYSIA AIRLINES MH370 · 7–8 MARCH 2014

Flight histories and terminal regions computed within the MH370 world model

After the aircraft left radar coverage, its satellite terminal kept exchanging link-maintenance messages with the Inmarsat ground station at Perth. Each exchange recorded a round-trip light-travel time (BTO) and a Doppler frequency offset (BFO). The project propagates a Boeing 777-200ER model through reanalysed weather and tests the saved histories under declared measurement and performance rules. The published six-history branch begins at the reconstructed 18:22:12 UTC radar handoff; its continued paths reach a model-support boundary or the weather floor, about 190 m WGS-84 ellipsoidal height. Other catalogue experiments report airborne final-transmission states without a continuation to the surface.

No part of this work locates the aircraft. Every computed aircraft coordinate is a conditional model state: it holds only under the assumptions stated with it. Observations, reanalysis fields and approximate geographic reference layers are identified separately. The published envelope covers exactly the stated model, incoming histories, response families and tests — it is not a claim that every mathematically possible BTO/BFO-compatible flight has been enumerated. Results later shown to be wrong are withdrawn in place, dated and explained, not silently replaced; the full revision history is public on GitHub.

Where this leaves the published histories. The early measurements allow many first legs besides the three behind the published histories, and with a climb after 19:41 those alternatives have the fuel. Carried through the two later turns to the end, these histories fit the specified timing measurements and admit modeled terminal responses compatible with the final frequency pair under the stated assumptions (which measurement plays which role). Across the tested first-hour variations, histories using the same later speed schedule remained in broadly similar parts of the arc, with differences in their exact positions. What spreads the positions is the speed schedule after 19:41, and the fuel does not fix it once the time of the climb is free. Across the existing catalogue and the new sample, saved final-transmission positions extend approximately from 31.5°S to 34.0°S. What the Mach 0.84 tests show

Mach 0.84 is a normal cruise speed for this aircraft and is used in many published reconstructions. The results below depend on the heights, speed schedules and turn commands that were computed. The complete-flight catalogue uses one cruise level after 19:41. The separate alternative-start stage now tests selected histories through 19:41; it does not extend the final-transmission catalogue.

  • The fuel available in the earlier complete histories. At the 19:41 arc those histories carry 26.3 to 27.0 t of fuel at a total mass of about 201 t. For the engines to run until the final transmissions at 00:19:29, that fuel has to last 4 h 38 min: an average of about 5.8 t per hour.
  • The height flown after 19:41. In the experiments carried beyond 19:41, no flight cruises higher than the 331.7 hPa pressure level, about 8.9 km (pressure altitude about 27,800 ft). Higher cruise levels, where the pressure is lower than 331.7 hPa, have not been flown after 19:41. That level is where the retained histories were when their climb was stopped at 18:42 (next item). After 19:41 the experiments varied the speed and the time of the climb to this level; they did not test any higher level.
  • What the earlier stages tested, and how far each was carried. Height and speed were varied in several earlier experiments. Their conclusions apply to the tested commands and measurement times. Selected alternative starts now reach 19:41; their continuation to the later measurements remains untested.

None of the 45 that continued the climb was supported to 19:41: at Mach 0.67 and 0.75 the model’s lift limit was reached; the Mach 0.84 cases exceeded the available thrust while still accelerating through about Mach 0.74 and climbing, before reaching Mach 0.84. That is the outcome of one command sequence (speed and height changed together). With the level held, all 15 at Mach 0.67, 10 of 15 at Mach 0.75 and 1 of 15 at Mach 0.84 reached 19:41; the other Mach 0.84 and 0.75 cases remained supported until they left the area covered by the model’s geometry, which is a limit of the calculation and not a physical event (record).
    • The earlier complete histories after 19:41: their arrivals are at three heights (365.1, 400 and 331.7 hPa), and the time of the climb, including no climb, was varied. The destination of every climb was the same level, 331.7 hPa; the cruise level was not a search variable.
    • 18:42, speed first and climb afterwards (12 November): 432 histories at Mach 0.60 to 0.86 and seven target levels. With the speed change made first, the model supports higher levels as the speed rises: about 27,800 ft at Mach 0.64 to 0.67, 30,100 ft at 0.68 to 0.70, 34,000 ft at 0.74 to 0.75, 35,000 ft at 0.76 to 0.80 and 37,000 ft at 0.82 to 0.86; nothing at 39,000 ft or above. All 18 histories at Mach 0.84 and 0.86 at or below 37,000 ft ended at the edge of the area the early-flight model covers, between about 19:30 and 19:41, and have no prediction for the 19:41 measurement; their support is shown only for the part computed. The low cruise level of the retained histories is therefore not forced by the flight model. This was a test of lift, thrust and fuel only: no satellite measurement was fitted and nothing was computed after 19:41 (record).
    • Not yet done: carrying the 108 accepted alternative-start histories beyond 19:41; continuing the 755 unselected histories accepted at 18:40 or testing the other accepted turn times; fitting the 19:41 measurement for the faster and higher histories of the speed-first test that left the modelled area; and testing any cruise level higher than the 331.7 hPa level (lower pressure) after 19:41. Gentler turns for the 34,000 ft starts and a slower acceleration to Mach 0.84 from the four 30,000 ft parents also remain untested.

  • The aircraft manual says the same. In the long-range cruise table of a flight manual for this airframe and engine type (transcribed table), the economical cruise speed at 200 t is Mach 0.72 to 0.75 at 27,000 to 29,000 ft and reaches Mach 0.84 only at 37,000 ft and above. At 180 t it is Mach 0.69 to 0.71 at 27,000 to 29,000 ft and reaches Mach 0.84 at 39,000 ft. The compatible speeds found here, Mach 0.63 to 0.71, lie at or below the economical speed for the height flown. Mach 0.84 at this height is well above it, which is why the fuel does not last.
  • What this does not show. It does not show that a Mach 0.84 flight is impossible. On the same manual table, long-range cruise at 37,000 to 39,000 ft burns about 6.1 t per hour at 200 t, falling to about 5.6 t per hour at 180 t, which is close to the 5.8 t per hour available. A complete history that gains speed first and then climbs to those heights, or that climbs to them after 19:41, and holds Mach 0.84 has not been computed. The speed-first test reached higher levels but its Mach 0.84 histories left the modelled area before the 19:41 measurement. The new alternative-start stage tests held levels through 19:41, not a higher cruise profile or a complete flight. Its Mach 0.84 frequency result is conditional on those levels and commands; no later endurance or final-transmission location follows from it.

The manual figures are steady-cruise values for a standard atmosphere and are quoted for comparison only; they are not a computed flight. The model’s own fuel flow was compared with this table and found about 1 % lower on average over the relevant masses and heights (between 0 and 2.4 % lower) (comparison).

Where the evidence stands

  • The six histories are one branch of a larger compatible family. Under the frozen model family (Mach 0.75 upstream, 0.67 downstream, λff = 1.005, prescribed lift-and-bank response) 98 response identities reproduce the final transmissions, and the 68 that glide on end in four bank-direction lobes between roughly 32.96–33.55°S and 94.36–96.29°E at 00:25–00:27 UTC. The closure tests of 10 November add a second speed family at the final transmissions and show that the first leg and the turn south were far less constrained than the earlier sweep indicated. These are possible terminal regions for the branch that was computed — not the envelope of all compatible flights, and not a verdict.
  • No aerodynamic path reaches the sea surface in the computation. Every continued flight is reported at the weather floor (≈190 m); the last 1–5 km along track are labelled kinematic projection, never computed contact.
  • A proposed location at 32.355°S, 97.4°E is on the record awaiting verification. Its originating run has not been located; it is listed as a point only, attributed to no history and not drawn on the catalogue maps, and two frozen-method experiments are planned before any claim is made.
  • The earlier satellite-and-flight chapter is archived. Its negations stand (northern commanded flights to 23.9–26.4°S; the long glide); its point-fit claims were withdrawn after the October corrections. The record is in the Archive of attempts and the closing report.
  • No drift source region is determinable from the real finds: the answer moves 1,000–1,900 km when an unmeasured assumption changes. The drift chapter remains open.

Reading the population labels

Different counts name different things. 6 incoming histories are complete flights from the radar handoff. 98 baseline response identities are the terminal responses compatible with the final transmissions. 128 displayed Mach-0.89 records are the 98 baseline states plus 24 exploratory command alternatives plus 6 reproduction controls. 196 final-transmission states are two samples (00:19:29 and 00:19:37) of each baseline response. 424 registered candidates are the continuation arms; 68 of them reach the terminal reporting endpoint, 332 stop at the model-support boundary (which does not by itself negate the flight hypothesis), and 24 are registered exploratory alternatives, not continued beyond their saved Mach 0.89 endpoints.
