Claude's assessment AI model capability test

Ryan asked the AI model that directed and audited this work for its own conclusion. This page records the answer as given in chat on 10 November 2026, by Claude (session configured as claude-fable-5-2). It is a model's reading of the evidence assembled in this project. It is not a finding of the investigation, not a computed probability, and not a basis for search planning without independent review.

Why this page exists. It is a test of what a language model can conclude when given close to a full repository of public MH370 data, equipment data and its own executed calculations. Read it as that test's output, alongside Ryan's assessment, which is separate and his own.

1. How the flight ended: high confidence

The aircraft ran out of engine fuel at about 00:17:30 UTC and was in a steep, accelerating descent at 00:19:37, close to the 7th arc in the southern Indian Ocean. Every calculation in this project agrees with that, and so does the official reading of the final frequency pair.

2. Where I would point: a region, not a coordinate

If the six flight histories are right, my best single region is the neighbourhood of the two final-transmission clusters: roughly 32.8–33.2°S, 95.0–95.5°E, within about 20 km of those states.

I weight that above the glide lobes mapped 65–80 km farther out, for three reasons:

  • 332 of the 392 nominal response arms end in the high-speed descent within seconds of the last transmission. Only 65 glide on.
  • The glide requires the more favourable of two unvalidated drag formulations, a prescribed roll-out, and a control law held after all electrical power is gone.
  • The eastern lobes clear the model limit by margins smaller than the project's own measured sensitivity.

3. How much I believe the region itself: moderate at best

My rough judgment is that I would not put it above about one in four that the wreck lies in this 33°S segment at all. That figure is a judgment, not a calculation. The reasons:

  • The satellite data do not fix position along the arc to better than several degrees. The earlier chapter of this project could not separate 32°S from 36°S.
  • The six histories are one self-consistent family under chosen speed schedules, not an enumeration of every compatible flight.
  • The fuel timing is a consistency check at the two-minute level, not confirmation.
  • The drift work found no determinable source region, so it neither supports nor contradicts this.
  • Parts of this area have documented sonar coverage. It was confirmed for 78 of 602 sampled locations; the rasters needed to resolve the rest could not be obtained.

4. What I give no weight

The proposed location at 32.355°S, 97.4°E has no supporting record yet, so I give it no weight until the location-unconstrained reconstruction runs. The breakup-and-flotation sequence is untested by anything built here.

What would change this conclusion

  • Verified seabed coverage at the cluster locations. If the seabed there was imaged at good quality and reviewed, this region is largely negated.
  • The location-unconstrained reconstruction. If freeing the turn near Penang sends compatible flights elsewhere, the 33°S family loses its standing.
  • Validated high-speed aerodynamics. This decides whether the glide lobes are physical at all.

The durable result of the project, in my view, is not the location. It is that every step from the radar handoff to the terminal states is connected, reproducible and open to attack. 239 people were aboard, and a traceable regional result with stated limits serves them better than a confident coordinate would.
