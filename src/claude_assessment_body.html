Revised 10–11 November 2026, after the closure tests. I ran the tests that the coverage audit called for. They show that the six histories are one branch of a much larger family of flights that satisfy the same early measurements. Sections 1, 2 and 3 below were written before that and are kept for the record; this box supersedes them.
  • After the first closure tests I withdrew the regional conclusion. Level or descending flight after 18:22, a turn south at any time from 18:30 to 18:36 and any course from 165° to 195° all satisfied the measurements through 18:39:58, and a sample reached the 19:41 arc.
  • The fuel tests cut both ways. Flown low to the end, those branches ran out of fuel before the final transmissions. Given a climb to the published height after 19:41 they did not, and the time of the climb, which nothing measures, moved the fuel-exhaustion time across the condition the published histories were selected on. The published histories are one of several branches that fit the measurements and the fuel.
  • The alternatives lead to broadly similar parts of the arc. Two climbed branches, carried through the two later turns to fuel exhaustion and the descent, fit the specified timing measurements and admit modeled terminal responses compatible with the final frequency pair under the stated assumptions. Across the tested first-hour variations, histories using the same later speed schedule remained in broadly similar parts of the arc, with differences in their exact positions.
  • I point to a stretch of the arc, not to the published clusters. The fuel does not select one speed schedule, so the stretch is set by the range of schedules sampled. Across the existing catalogue and the new sample, saved final-transmission positions extend approximately from 31.5°S to 34.0°S. The northern and southern saved extremes are about 385 km apart at 00:19:37 UTC, and the new southernmost state is about 55 km from the previous southernmost state. These are great-circle separations between saved end states (earlier response records, new extremes), not lengths along the satellite arc, and they do not imply that every point between has a supported history. Updated 12 November: 39 further combinations (22 targeted on four arrivals, 17 completions) moved the verified northern extreme to 30.90°S, 97.43°E (88 km from the previous one) and the southern to 34.06°S, 93.97°E (5 km); the two are about 478 km apart, and the northern state is compatible only under the terminal warm-up assumption; within any one of the four terminal treatments the checked northern and southern states are 478.3 to 478.4 km apart (findings). Updated later on 12 November: a separate run at target Mach 0.650 to 0.630 on the two northern arrivals, under a lower speed rule (realised Mach at or above 0.55), found compatible responses at every speed on the descending arrival, reaching 29.88°S, 98.35°E at Mach 0.630, 144 km beyond the previous northern extreme and verified at half step; a response 470 m from it is compatible under all four terminal treatments and is also verified (findings). The states moved north with each reduction in speed and had not stopped at the lowest speed computed. Updated 13 November: a follow-on run on the same arrival, at Mach 0.625 down to 0.590, reached 28.95°S, 99.14°E at Mach 0.610 (verified at half step, with a response 0.35 km away compatible under all four treatments). From Mach 0.605 to 0.590 every combination computed, including the latest climb and no climb, still has fuel after the final transmissions, so in the grid computed this family of solutions ends between the sampled speeds Mach 0.610 and 0.605 (findings). That is the end of one family: one arrival, one cruise level, one fuel-flow value, and fuel exhaustion before the final transmissions. The last two combinations (Mach 0.590) were completed after I changed my own stated stopping rule with the other 23 results in view; no acceptance rule changed, and the change is recorded in the method. A finite grid is not a proof about every speed or turn solution. I do not regard this as a northern limit on the aircraft’s position, and the southern end has not been tested in the same way. A different quantity: measured along one fixed satellite-model timing arc, the 11 November sample alone spans about 307 km at 00:19:37. Nothing I have computed ranks one part of it above another.
  • My estimate that the wreck lies in that stretch is about one in five. It is my subjective judgment. It is not an output of the admissibility calculation, which leaves the computed cases unranked. I derive no figure for the published clusters from their share of the plotted cases or of the extent: counts, map density and area depend on how the sampling was designed.
  • Speed limits of the 11 November sample. The Mach 0.65 trials stopped because the calculated air-relative speed fell slightly below an inherited minimum-Mach acceptance rule. The native performance checks passed at the stopping points. This does not establish the aircraft’s minimum speed or demonstrate a complete compatible flight at Mach 0.65 (diagnostic findings). The highest target Mach producing a compatible history in this sample was 0.71. In the original sample one trial was flown for each faster configuration; each exhausted fuel before 00:11 and was not continued by the powered-through-00:11 fitting procedure. A follow-up examined ten further second-turn configurations for the published arrival at Mach 0.72, with the first turn held fixed: all exhausted fuel 7 to 16 s before 00:11. Other allowed turn solutions, the other arrivals and intermediate speeds remain unresolved.
  • Fuel timing (11 November; supersedes section 1). Within the tested fuel-exhaustion-triggered response family, compatible cases place the engine-fuel event approximately 11 seconds to eight minutes before log-on. That interval is conditional on the response family; it is not independent proof that fuel exhaustion triggered the real terminal descent. Thirteen sampled combinations still had fuel at the final transmissions: they were not rejected by the fitted timing constraints but were outside the terminal response construction used here, and their alternative terminal behaviour remains untested.
  • The held-back measurement. The 22:41 observation remains withheld from fitting and selection for these branches. The candidate population and its predictions will be frozen before any comparison, and later revisions informed by that result will be identified as such, since they are no longer a blind check.
  • What would move it further: the other 19 arrivals at the new speeds; a review of the inherited minimum-Mach rule that stopped Mach 0.65; a systematic search for turn solutions above Mach 0.71 on every arrival; Mach 0.84 flown at cruise heights of 35,000 ft and above, which these experiments did not vary (why Mach 0.84 is not among the compatible flights); other climb rates and heights; other fuel-flow values; terminal behaviour with fuel remaining; and validated drag and control assumptions for the glides. If these converge on the same stretch, the region stands on firmer ground; if one leads elsewhere, it does not.

Record: closure tests.

1. How the flight ended superseded 11 November 2026

Kept as written on 10 November. Superseded: the time of fuel exhaustion is no longer a single value, and “high confidence” is withdrawn for it; see the fuel-timing item in the revision box.

The aircraft ran out of engine fuel at about 00:17:30 UTC and was in a steep, accelerating descent at 00:19:37, close to the 7th arc in the southern Indian Ocean. Every calculation in this project agrees with that, and so does the official reading of the final frequency pair.

2. Where I would point: a region, not a coordinate superseded

If the six flight histories are right, my best single region is the neighbourhood of the two final-transmission clusters: roughly 32.8–33.2°S, 95.0–95.5°E, within about 20 km of those states.

I weight that above the glide lobes mapped 65–80 km farther out, for three reasons:

  • 332 of the 392 nominal response arms end in the high-speed descent within seconds of the last transmission. Only 65 glide on.
  • The glide requires the more favourable of two unvalidated drag formulations, a prescribed roll-out, and a control law held after all electrical power is gone.
  • The eastern lobes clear the model limit by margins smaller than the project's own measured sensitivity.

3. How much I believe the region itself: moderate at best superseded

My rough judgment is that I would not put it above about one in five that the wreck lies in this 33°S segment at all (revised down from one in four after the coverage audit of 10 November). That figure is a judgment, not a calculation. The reasons:

  • The satellite data do not fix position along the arc to better than several degrees. The earlier chapter of this project could not separate 32°S from 36°S.
  • The six histories are one self-consistent family under chosen speed schedules, not an enumeration of every compatible flight.
  • The fuel timing is not evidence at all. The six histories were selected because their fuel exhaustion lines up with the log-on, under a fuel-flow value with no independent calibration; 18 other candidates were set aside by that condition.
  • The sweep is not closed. Several defining quantities sit at the edge of the tested range, and the starting state near Penang was never varied.
  • The drift work found no determinable source region, so it neither supports nor contradicts this.
  • Parts of this area have documented sonar coverage. It was confirmed for 78 of 602 sampled locations; the rasters needed to resolve the rest could not be obtained.

4. What I give no weight

The proposed location at 32.355°S, 97.4°E has no supporting record yet, so I give it no weight until the location-unconstrained reconstruction runs. The breakup-and-flotation sequence is untested by anything built here.

What would change this conclusion

  • Verified seabed coverage at the cluster locations. If the seabed there was imaged at good quality and reviewed, this region is largely negated.
  • The location-unconstrained reconstruction. If freeing the turn near Penang sends compatible flights elsewhere, the 33°S family loses its standing.
  • Validated high-speed aerodynamics. This decides whether the glide lobes are physical at all.

The durable result of the project, in my view, is not the location. It is that every step from the radar handoff to the terminal states is connected, reproducible and open to attack. 239 people were aboard, and a traceable regional result with stated limits serves them better than a confident coordinate would.
