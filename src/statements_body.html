STATEMENTS AND THEIR SCOPE · RECORDS TO 26 NOVEMBER 2026

What this project can state, and exactly how far each statement reaches

Elsewhere on this site the work is organised by experiment. This page is organised by claim. Each entry is written to be as firm as the evidence allows and no firmer, and each one carries the conditions that make it true. Nothing here is a location for the aircraft.

Why there is no word like "proven" on this page. Every statement below is conditional on something: a measurement set, a flight model, an ocean product, a search that covered particular cases. A statement with its conditions removed would be a different and weaker claim. So each entry has three parts: the statement, the scope that bounds it, and what would overturn it. If you quote one, quote the scope with it.

A. Geometry and timing

These follow from the published Inmarsat measurements and the satellite's position. No flight model, aircraft performance figure or ocean product enters them.

A1 · The 7th arc ring established

At 00:19:29 the aircraft was on a circle of angular radius 44.4701° about the sub-satellite point at 0.5345°N, 64.4641°E. On that circle, 90.400°E corresponds to 36.889°S, 93.094°E to 35.000°S, 95.484°E to 33.000°S and 98.510°E to 29.900°S.

Scope. The ring is the project's existing definition, carried unchanged since the satellite model was frozen. It is a circle on a sphere at a stated height; the small shift with aircraft altitude is not folded in here. It fixes a line, not a point on that line. An independent recomputation in the drift work returned 90.40°E at 36.89°S, and the area work reproduced 36.889°S at 90.40°E.

Would be overturned by. An error in the published satellite ephemeris or in the timing bias calibration. The calibration's largest benchmark residual is 59 µs against a 60 µs tolerance, so it is not a comfortable margin.

Record. Satcom, Canonical data.

A2 · Movement between 22:41 and 00:11 established

Between the 22:41 and 00:11 exchanges the aircraft moved at least about 530 km in a straight line, an average of at least about 190 knots over the interval. The aircraft-to-satellite range grew by about 565 km. The satellite itself moved about 397 km in Earth-fixed coordinates, but for any fixed aircraft position on the 22:41 ring, sampled at 23 heights from sea level to 43,000 ft, that motion could lengthen the range by at most about 33 km. The triangle inequality gives the remainder.

Scope. It rules out the aircraft staying near its 22:41 position. It does not give a heading, a total distance flown, or any separation between the modelled routes, all of which exceed it. This calculation uses the 22:41 timing observation, which the rest of the project withholds from fitting; that use is disclosed. The 397 km satellite displacement was confirmed here against the project's satellite-state table; the 33 km figure was computed by Ryan Hall with a separate assistant and has not been independently recomputed.

Would be overturned by. An error in the 22:41 or 00:11 timing, or an error in the 33 km bound large enough to matter — it would have to be wrong by hundreds of kilometres.

Record. Overview. Comparable minimum-speed limits between rings appear in published analyses, so this is a consistency result and adds no location.

A3 · A position must be on the ring, not near it established

A candidate position that is not on the 7th arc is not compatible with the 00:19:29 timing, and the distances involved are large. As a worked example, 31.4°S 90.4°E — a point that recurs in drift discussion because of its arrival timing — lies 464 km inside the ring. At that latitude the ring runs through 97.14°E.

Scope. Pure geometry against A1. It says nothing about whether any particular latitude on the ring is right. A position off the ring is only admissible if the ring itself, or the timing behind it, is wrong.

Would be overturned by. The same things that would overturn A1.

Record. Recomputed for this page from the ring definition in A1.

B. Where the tested flights go

These describe the flights the project has actually flown. They are bounded by the flight types tested: a flight type not yet flown could go elsewhere. None of them is a statement about the aircraft.

B1 · The 19:41 crossing established within the flights flown

Every accepted flight that turns south between 18:40 and 19:41 meets the 19:41 ring between 2.10°S and 1.27°N — a stretch of about 375 km. 700 distinct arrivals from 102 starting flights across all 13 families of earlier flight history fall inside it. A related result: all three tested turn times produce accepted arrivals, so the measurements constrain where the turn delivers the aircraft far more tightly than they constrain when the turn was made or how sharp it was.

Scope. 102 of 809 accepted 18:40:12 states; turns at 18:40:12, 19:00 and 19:20 only; Mach 0.62, 0.67, 0.72, 0.78 and 0.84; one turn, then a single course solved to put the flight on the ring within one microsecond, with frequency within 7 Hz. The narrow longitude range (93.49–93.75°E) follows from being on the ring and carries no information. 700 arrivals from related starting flights are not 700 independent confirmations.

Would be overturned by. A different reconstruction of the flight before 18:40, which has not been tested; or a turn time outside the three tested; or more than one turn before 19:41.

Record. Crossing and endings.

B2 · Where fully tested flights end established within the flights flown

Every flight on file that satisfies the satellite timing at 20:41, 21:41 and 00:11, the aircraft performance model, the fuel, and the two final transmissions is on arc 7 between 29.9°S and 37.9°S at 00:19:37 — 221 flights and 3,245 compatible end states, all within 21 km of the arc, 3.8 to 11.1 km above the surface.

Scope. Only the flight types flown are represented: great-circle steering after 19:41, the speeds and levels of five runs, and the starting points of those runs. The 22:41 measurements are withheld from acceptance throughout. A continuation carrying 74 further 19:41 arrivals to arc 7 completed its search on 26 November 2026 and returned 89 accepted flights; those are not in the counts above and are being folded in.

Would be overturned by. A flight type not yet flown that satisfies the same tests and ends outside the range — for example a profile with more turns, a speed schedule not tested, or a different reconstruction before 19:41.

Record. Crossing and endings.

B3 · Every compatible ending is a rapid descent established within the responses modelled

No modelled end-of-flight response that matches the 00:19:29 and 00:19:37 frequency readings is in level flight. Across all 3,245 compatible end states the descent rate at 00:19:37 is between 69 and 121 m/s — roughly 13,600 to 23,800 ft/min. The slowest of them is still far steeper than any normal descent.

Scope. This is a property of the end-of-flight response model and the final two frequency measurements, not of the whole flight. It describes the aircraft's state at 00:19:37, and says nothing about what happened afterwards: a descent that was later arrested is not excluded by it. The frequency readings themselves are the weakest-held part of the measurement set.

Would be overturned by. Setting the final two frequency readings aside, which the project tests as a separate scenario; or an end-of-flight response outside the modelled family.

Record. Terminal responses, Crossing and endings.

B4 · If the descent continued, almost nothing is unsearched rests on an unverified coverage record

If every fully tested flight continued its 00:19:37 descent to the sea, 99.8 per cent of the area they reach is either documented sonar coverage or inside the band Ocean Infinity is reported to have searched in 2018. The area is 43,279 km²; 17,245 km² is documented, 25,956 km² is reported-but-unverified, and 78 km² is outside both.

Scope. "Searched" means sonar data exists, not that wreckage would have been detected; data gaps, low-quality lines and terrain shadows inside the layers have not been located. The reported 2018 band (22 nautical miles either side of the arc, 25–36°S) is taken from an independent analyst's published account; the project holds no geometry for it and the 36°S southern limit is an assumption. The 2025–26 search is not mapped at all.

Would be overturned by. The actual Ocean Infinity coverage and data-quality maps. This is the single most useful missing input in the project.

Record. Crossing and endings.

B5 · If the descent was arrested, a strip beyond the arc is unsearched conditional on a glide

If the aircraft recovered from the descent and glided roughly the way it was moving, about 111,595 km² of the reachable area lies outside both coverage layers, and 107,586 km² of that is beyond the arc — farther from the satellite. It forms two strips along the arc: about 61,000 km² between 29 and 35°S, and about 50,600 km² between 35 and 40°S.

Scope. Three assumptions, none of them tested: a lift-to-drag ratio of 17, still air, and that a recovery continues within 45° of the track at 00:19:37. That track is a moment inside a banked turn, not a settled heading. The inner edge of the strip is the unverified 2018 band from B4, and the 2025–26 search may cover part of the southern strip. This site does not assert that the aircraft glided. The statement is a conditional and nothing in the project's own results establishes its condition.

Would be overturned by. The actual coverage maps; or evidence about the end of the flight that settles whether a recovery occurred.

Record. Crossing and endings.

B6 · Mach 0.84 held after 19:41 negated for the arrivals tested

For the ten Mach 0.84 arrivals tested, no flight holding that speed after 19:41 meets the satellite timing at 20:41, 21:41 and 00:11. Two results combine. With a first turn of up to 20° either side of the arrival course, no flight was accepted: 80 searches, 532 flights. Every left-turn search that found no starting point was still closing on the 20:41 measurement at 20°, missing by 8 to 186 km of range, and every right turn made the miss larger. An earlier grid in this project had already established the 20° corridor from the satellite data: Mach 0.84 flights turning more than 20° did not meet the later measurements. Inside the corridor the speed misses 20:41; outside it, it misses the rest.

Scope. Ten arrivals, one for each crossing-latitude and height class among 32 distinct Mach 0.84 arrivals, crossing the 19:41 ring between 2.1°S and 0.5°S; four cruise levels each; speed held constant from 19:41. In the two arrivals where a starting point was found inside the corridor, every trial flight ran out of fuel before 00:11. The earlier grid was run in October 2026 by Ryan Hall with Codex; its record sits in a working thread and is not yet filed in the project repository, and it has not been confirmed that it used these same crossing positions. The nearest misses inside the corridor are 8 to 11 km of range against a 6.4 km band, which this model cannot resolve by themselves.

Would be overturned by. A Mach 0.84 flight from a different 19:41 crossing, or one that changes speed after 19:41; or the earlier grid's record, once filed, showing it did not cover this case.

Record. Crossing and endings.

C. Ocean transport

From the debris-drift runs. No satellite measurement enters these; the arc is used only as a place to release objects. The current statements come from the coastal-arrival run of 21 October 2026, the first experiment in this project in which reaching a coast is a recorded physical event rather than a discarded particle.

C1 · The African rim receives far more than Australia all four reanalyses agree

Material released along arc 7 reaches the African and Madagascan rim between 5 and 15 times more often than it reaches Australia, in every one of four independently built ocean reanalyses. Per field: ORAS5 14.6, GLORYS2V4 10.7, GLORYS12V1 5.8, C-GLORS 5.0. Nothing was tuned to produce it — the release design, the slip coefficient and the region boxes were all fixed before the run.

Scope. 420 objects per field released 8 March 2014 and run to 31 August 2018, from 14 latitudes between 24.5 and 43°S. A coastal arrival is a land-boundary intersection under the declared beaching policy, or a velocity failure within a declared 60 km halo of a mapped coast — the second is a candidate landfall at the ocean model's own resolution and is never described as a verified beaching. It dominates by an order of magnitude. The ratio is an upper bound, because the coastline product is clipped at 130°E: only Australia's west and south-west coast exists in the model, so Australian arrivals are undercounted everywhere. Region boxes are a declared assumption of the analysis, not data. The beaching policy's production default is never-refloat; its four sensitivity policies were not run, and there is no stranding probability or beach residence model. One object class, one release instant, generic 1.2 per cent windage.

What it is not. The observational record shows recovery on the African rim and nothing on Western Australian coasts, and the ratio is consistent with that — but Western Australia's coast is sparsely populated and was never searched the way Réunion, Mozambique and Madagascar were after July 2015. Absence of recovery there is not absence of arrival, so this is not confirmation of the debris record.

Would be overturned by. Unclipped shoreline data east of 130°E, which would also let Australia be counted properly; or an object-specific windage response in place of the generic coefficient.

Record. Four years.

C2 · Where material goes turns over near 38°S reproduced by all four fields

North of about 38°S, released material goes to the African rim almost exclusively. Between 38 and 41°S, Australia takes over. South of 41°S, the measurement does not exist.

Scope. Same run and same limits as C1, 30 objects per latitude per field. The southern band is unmeasured, not empty. The coastline product is clipped at 130°E and 53.2°S; objects released at 41, 42.5 and 43°S travel east out of that window and the model has no land to put them on. For ORAS5 and C-GLORS all 90 such objects end with no land coverage and their fate is unknown; GLORYS12V1 loses the same objects at the edge of its ocean field instead. The censor differs, the ignorance is the same.

Would be overturned by. The same acquisition — unclipped shoreline geometry east of 130°E. The sub-41°S question has now been blocked twice, first by a wind file and then by a coastline file, and remains open.

Record. Four years.

C3 · No field delivers to Réunion in time clean negative result

No ocean field puts material on Réunion by day 508, the date the flaperon was found there. Two of the four deliver nothing to Réunion at all across 420 objects; the two that do arrive on day 572 and day 1,551, both after the discovery. Madagascar is the opposite case and is reproduced comfortably: all four fields deliver between day 216 and day 577 against an upper bound of 827, with 49 to 112 arrivals each.

Scope, and why this is not evidence against an arc origin. Three readings stay open and this run does not choose between them. Réunion is a target of roughly 2,500 km²: GLORYS12V1 puts 12 objects on Mauritius, 220 km away, by day 503, so the Mascarene region is reached in time and Réunion specifically is not. The run uses the generic 1.2 per cent windage, not the flaperon's own measured response, which is larger and rotated — under that object-specific response the flaperon does reach Réunion in the project's separate reachability work. And 14 release latitudes with 420 objects is a coarse instrument. Arrival counts are not probabilities: a zero is not zero physical likelihood.

Would be overturned by. An object-specific windage run on this coastal machinery, which has not been done.

Record. Four years, Drift diagnostics.

C4 · Position across the arc hardly matters established within the sweep

Latitude along the arc outweighs position across the arc by between 7 and 61 times. Moving a release 130 km either side of the ring changes westward transport by 5.5 percentage points on average, against a latitude spread of 43 to 87 points. A 260 km band across the ring behaves as a single release.

Scope. The four-reanalysis arc-7 latitude sweep of 15 October 2026: three cross-arc positions at each of 14 latitudes, on the ring and 130 km either side. It means drift results cannot discriminate where across the arc an object started, so drift can speak to a latitude but never to a crossing position. The 15 October censoring audit examined this claim specifically and left it standing: a boundary that acts on all latitudes in the same direction does not create a gradient between them, and the curves correlate at r = +0.76 to +0.95 between models.

Would be overturned by. A finer release grid or a product with much stronger cross-arc structure.

Record. Four years.

D. Not tested and not run

D1 · The open list not computed

Listed so that silence is not read as a negative result.

  • A single constant course after 19:41. The search for one was paused after 66 cells with nothing accepted and a best miss of 152.6 km; the reference flights it was compared against contain further turns. Whether another single-course flight can be accepted remains open.
  • The 22:41 measurements. Withheld from acceptance throughout, so that they stay available as an independent check. The one place they are used — statement A2 — says so.
  • A sweep from the last radar contact to arc 7. Designed and written, never started.
  • The debris-source drift inversion. Designed and preregistered on 30 October 2026, tested on synthetic forcing, never run on real ocean data. It is the only test the project has that is independent of the flight modelling.
  • Ocean Infinity coverage geometry, 2018 and 2025–26. Not held. B4 and B5 both depend on it.
  • Unclipped shoreline geometry east of 130°E. Not held. Without it the drift work cannot measure the band south of 41°S at all, and cannot count Australian arrivals properly. See C2.
  • Detection. Nothing in this project models whether sonar would have detected the wreckage where data exists.
  • Any location for the aircraft. No statement on this page, and none elsewhere on this site, places it.

How these statements are maintained

Rules applies to the whole site

  • A result with a named open question is held back from this page until that question is answered.
  • A statement found to be flawed comes off this page entirely. The reason is recorded once, in the Archive of attempts, which is where the project keeps what it has retired. Nothing on this page is a result that is no longer held.
  • Where the model cannot resolve a difference, the result is reported as possible, not as a negation. A near miss is a near miss.
  • No model parameter is ever adjusted toward a wanted answer. Acceptance rules are fixed before a run and are not revised after seeing its output.
  • Scores and areas on this site are never probabilities. The size of an area says nothing about likelihood.
  • Errors made by the project, including by its AI collaborators, stay on the record — in the Archive of attempts, with the stopped runs preserved.
