Terminal responses of the accepted cruise flights

The accepted flights of the 16 November cruise catalogue meet the fitted measurements through 00:11 UTC, with every flight still airborne at that time; the catalogue’s continuation records extend to fuel exhaustion or 00:40. This experiment carries those flights to the two final satellite transmissions. After the modelled engine fuel runs out, a declared response is applied: the lift is reduced and the aircraft banks to 60°. The result is compared with the timing recorded at 00:19:29 and the frequencies recorded at both final transmissions, 00:19:29 and 00:19:37 UTC, and each compatible response is followed until the model can no longer support it. Computed 16–17 November 2026; reviewed and accepted with one recorded provenance exception.

These are conditional model states, not crash locations. Every position on this page holds only under the cruise flight, response shape and performance model stated with it. Together the positions form a conditional terminal envelope of the sampled responses. It is not a probability map and not an outer bound. Counts are counts of sampled responses, not probabilities. The {{flights_propagated}} flights share their fitted cruise measurements, so repeated outcomes are not independent confirmations. No path in this experiment reaches the sea surface.

The response that was tested

Before the response. From the fuel event the model has no thrust and holds the lift coefficient and bank angle it had at that moment.

The response. At a chosen onset time the lift coefficient falls to half its value and the bank goes to 60°, both over 10 s. Onset times were sampled every second from 60 s before to 8 s after 00:19:29 (69 values), with the bank to the left and to the right. That is 138 responses for each flight and {{responses}} responses in all. Each was propagated to 00:19:37 or to the first point where the performance model stops.

The fuel-flow multiplier (1.000), the fuel on board, the fuel-event time and every command before the event are those of the cruise catalogue, unchanged. No cruise flight was recomputed.

Timing (BTO) at 00:19:29Compared, not fitted. The residual is formed against 18,400 µs and reported against bands of 62, 124 and 186 µs: one, two and three standard deviations for this message type (Davey et al., 2016). The bands select nothing.

Earlier exposure of that measurement is disclosed; it is not described as unread.

A response is continued beyond 00:19:37 if the model supports it to 00:19:37 and it meets the frequency requirement under at least one of the four treatments. Timing does not enter that rule, so every response that could satisfy both measurements together is among those continued.

What can be read from the outcomes

These are interpretations of the computed outcomes above. They hold for this response family and these {{flights_propagated}} flights.

  1. In this response family, the two frequency measurements pick out a narrow range of onset times, and nearly the same one for every flight. A response of this shape meets a frequency treatment only when it begins {{continued_onset_last}} to {{continued_onset_first}} s before 00:19:29; without the warm-up allowance, {{fn_onset}} s before. The frequency depends mainly on vertical speed, the response shape fixes how quickly the descent steepens, and so the measurements fix when it began. A different response shape would give a different range. The result does not say that a response began then.
  2. The 00:19:29 timing does not separate anything here. All residuals are within {{timing_max_abs}} µs, so each joint match is decided by frequency alone. That all {{flights_propagated}} flights meet an unfitted measurement this closely is a consistency of the cruise catalogue with the seventh arc. It is one shared outcome, not {{flights_propagated}} confirmations.
  3. More than half of the sampled responses were not compared with the measurements at all. A response beginning early enough reaches the operating-speed limit first, where this stage of the model stops. Those {{out_before}} responses are neither supported nor contradicted by the transmissions. Earlier onsets are a limit of the model, not a mathematical exclusion.
  4. The geographic spread is set by the cruise flights, not by the terminal response. Each group forms one cluster {{sep_min_km}} to {{sep_max_km}} km long at 00:19:29, and with the bank held the path ends {{held_km}} km further on. Nothing is implied between the clusters.
  5. About half of the continued paths end in the air, where the model stops. The model gives no surface position for any path. An ending at the lowest weather level is still {{floor_height}} above the model surface.
  6. The electrical status differs by group. For one group the model’s APU reserve is gone before the log-on; for another it outlasts both transmissions by seconds to tens of seconds. Neither statement establishes what powered the satellite unit.

Limits

  • One law before the response, one response shape, one bank magnitude, two directions, onset times from −60 to +8 s at 1 s spacing. “No sampled response” is a statement about this family only.
  • The onset stage stops at the operating-speed limit, so the earliest onsets are not compared with the measurements.
  • The frequency test and its warm-up ranges are those of Holland (2017). Which treatment applies is not determined by this model.
  • The 00:19:37 timing is not used.
  • No surface position is computed. Last positions are last model states.
  • Cruise levels and speeds between the tested ones, later climbs and speed changes remain outside the cruise catalogue and therefore outside this result.
  • The {{flights_not_propagated}} flights whose engines outlast the transmissions were not propagated. Another electrical or control sequence could apply to them; none was examined.
  • The satellite-input sensitivity identified in the separate satellite audit remains open.
  • The 22:41 measurement was excluded from this experiment: no prediction, residual or comparison. It is not described as unread; the flight model loads the recorded event table, including that epoch, when it starts. The later 22:41 check finds all {{q_parents}} of these flights within its timing band. That adds a status to the flights and changes no result on this page: a flight that meets 22:41 does not make its responses meet the final transmissions.

Record of the run one provenance exception

Numerical checks. {{chk_onset}} onset responses and {{chk_cont}} continuations were repeated at half the integration step, and all met their criteria. The largest position differences were {{chk_onset_pos_mm}} mm and {{chk_cont_pos_mm}} mm; the largest differences in predicted frequency and timing were {{chk_freq_hz}} Hz and {{chk_timing_us}} µs. These measure the stability of the calculation, not the accuracy of the model for the real aircraft.

Cost. {{cost_h}} hours of computation were charged against declared allowances of {{cost_limit_h}} hours. No ceiling was reached and no calculation ended in an undefined way.

Permanent provenance exception. Flight {{prov_flight}}, {{prov_bank}} bank, onset {{prov_onset}} s after 00:19:29. {{prov_text}} The cause was a defect in the step that published the two workers’ files: lines appended by one worker during the other’s publication were overwritten. The journal of attempts is short by three lines as a result. This response met no frequency treatment, like all 180 responses at that onset time; it is not a continued response and takes part in no numerical check. Every count on this page is a count of the surviving records.

Review. An independent review of the published journal, account, manifest and check records found each of the 13,925 planned identities settled once, with the single irregularity above. The reviewer did not reread every stored response and trajectory; that each of the {{responses}} responses and {{out_continued}} continuations is present exactly once rests on the project’s own verification of the records. The frozen calculation and its raw records were left unchanged, and nothing was recomputed. A replacement for the publishing step has since been qualified separately; it has not yet been used for a production run.
