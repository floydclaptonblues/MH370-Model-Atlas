How an attempt can end

Four outcomes, and they are not interchangeable:

  • Negated under stated conditions. A validly executed test rejected the tested scenario under its stated assumptions. A negation is a result — it narrows the problem — and it holds only under its conditions.
  • Superseded. A later method, dataset or run replaced it. Supersession alone does not make the earlier result wrong.
  • Invalidated or withdrawn. Either an identified defect made particular outputs unreliable, in which case the defect and the affected conclusions are stated, or an item was withdrawn for a documented change of scope with no defect found. The entry says which.
  • Operationally abandoned. Work stopped for a practical reason — compute, data access, architecture. Its scientific question may remain open.

Reaching a model-support boundary does not establish that the underlying flight hypothesis was negated, and "possibly corrupted" stays an unresolved-review label until an actual defect is identified.
