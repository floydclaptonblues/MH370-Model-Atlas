The 22:41 measurement and the 266 cruise histories

The 16 November cruise catalogue holds 266 model flights that were built to match the satellite timing recorded at 20:41, 21:41 and 00:11 UTC. One more exchange with the satellite was recorded between those times, at 22:41:22 UTC. It was not used to build or select any of the flights. This page reports what happens when each flight’s prediction for that moment is set beside the recorded value. The project calls this a withheld-measurement consistency check with disclosed prior exposure, and says below what that name does and does not claim.

These are counts of related model flights, not probabilities, and nothing here is a location. The 266 flights share their starting arrivals, their fitted measurements and one model. Flights of one incoming speed and cruise level have nearly the same prediction, so they tend to share one outcome and are not independent tests. A model state in the air at 22:41 is not where the aircraft was, and it says nothing about where the flight ended.

Result 18 November 2026

At 22:41:22 UTC the ground station recorded a burst timing offset of {{q_obs_timing}} µs and a burst frequency offset of {{q_obs_freq}} Hz. The timing offset measures the time the signal took to travel from the aircraft to the satellite and on to the ground, and so fixes the distance between aircraft and satellite at that moment: one microsecond corresponds to about {{q_m_per_us}} m. The criterion is the one that accepted these flights at the fitted times: the recorded timing minus the predicted timing must be within ±{{q_band}} µs, about {{q_band_km}} km of that distance. It was fixed in writing before any prediction for 22:41 existed.

The edge of the band passes through one group of flights, and a few microseconds separate the two outcomes within it. The outcomes themselves are numerically firm: computed at half the integration step, every prediction moves by at most {{q_step_timing}} µs, and no outcome changes.

Outside the band does not mean shown to be impossible. That firmness belongs to the arithmetic. The recorded timing is not accurate to a few microseconds, and neither are the model’s inputs: the satellite’s position, the calibration, the winds, the stored commands. A flight {{q_exc_min}} µs outside the band and one {{q_in_margin_min}} µs inside it are not distinguished by this measurement in any physical sense. What can be said is that {{q_out}} flights do not meet the declared criterion at 22:41 and {{q_meet}} do. The {{q_out}} are not negated, and the {{q_meet}} are not confirmed.

Two further thresholds were named beforehand for description only: {{q_within29}} of {{q_all}} flights are within ±29 µs and all {{q_within129}} within ±129 µs. They do not alter the outcome at ±{{q_band}} µs. A smaller residual does not rank one flight above another.

How the check was run

  1. The method was written and fixed first. The population (all 266 flights), the recorded event and its fields, the sign of the residual, the criterion and the wording of each possible outcome were set down before any prediction for 22:41 was formed. The program was reviewed, and faults found in review were repaired, before it was allowed to run.
  2. A gate on eleven flights. One flight of each combination was flown again from its stored commands. Each reproduced its catalogue record exactly, and the routine that forms a prediction gave the catalogue’s own stored values at 21:41 and 00:11. Nothing was evaluated at 22:41. The gate was run twice, once for each of two revisions of the program: a fault in its record-keeping was repaired between them, and a gate qualifies only the program it ran.
  3. Predictions for all 266 flights. Every flight was flown again and reproduced its record. One prediction of timing and frequency at 22:41:22 was then formed at the catalogue’s integration step and one at half that step: {{q_evals}} evaluations in all, none repeated. The two steps agree to within {{q_step_timing}} µs and {{q_step_freq}} Hz. The predictions were fixed, with a hash, before the recorded value was selected.
  4. The comparison. Only then was the recorded value selected and subtracted. The comparison was made by the project’s independent reviewer (Codex, an AI model that also reviewed each earlier step), with the program fixed in step 1, and again with a second program written separately from the method in 50-digit decimal arithmetic. The two agree on all {{q_all}} outcomes. The first program, run again on the computing machine, gave the same files byte for byte.

The predicted timings span {{q_pred_spread}} µs across the 266 flights ({{q_pred_min}} to {{q_pred_max}} µs), less than the {{q_band_full}} µs width of the band. That was known from the predictions alone, before the comparison: the check could at most separate some flights from others, and most of the spread, {{q_pred_between}} µs, lies between combinations and not within them.

What “withheld” means here, and the limits

  • Withheld from the fit, not unknown. The 22:41 timing entered no search, adjustment or selection that produced these flights, and the cruise run’s guard recorded no request for a prediction there. But the value is public and is shown elsewhere in this Atlas; earlier experiments of this project formed predictions at 22:41 and compared other flights with it; the people and models who chose this catalogue’s speeds, levels and calibration worked after those comparisons existed; and the flight model loads the whole recorded event table, 22:41 included, whenever it starts. The runs of this check that did so are logged. It is not a test against a measurement nobody had seen, and that the catalogue’s design was uninfluenced by earlier 22:41 results cannot be shown.
  • An interpolation along a fitted leg. Each flight already matches the timing at 21:41 and at 00:11. Its last commanded turn starts between 20:56 and 21:26; after it the flight holds its commanded Mach number and pressure level with no further commanded turn. Held Mach is not constant ground speed, since temperature and wind change along the leg, but a prediction partway along a leg that is matched at both ends is constrained by both. The check mainly tests whether the commanded speed and level, the weather fields and the satellite model carry a flight correctly across that leg. Agreement at 22:41 is weaker evidence than agreement at a time the flights were not built around.
  • One model, fixed inputs. One satellite ephemeris and calibration, one wind and temperature field, the stored commands. No uncertainty is attached to a prediction. The spread across flights is a spread across accepted histories, not an error bar. The sensitivity to the satellite inputs identified in the separate satellite audit remains open.
  • Exact reproduction is local. Every flight reproduced its record to the last digit on the machine and numerical libraries that built the catalogue. That is not a claim about another machine.
  • Conditional states. At 22:41:22 the 266 model flights are in the air between {{q_lat_range}} and {{q_lon_range}}. Those are states of model flights at one moment. They are not an estimate of the aircraft’s position and not a search area.
  • Nothing about the end of the flight. The check concerns the cruise. It does not test the final transmissions, the descent or an impact position.
