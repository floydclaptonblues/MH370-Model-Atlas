The 22:41 measurement and the 266 cruise histories

The 16 November cruise catalogue holds 266 model flights that were built to match the satellite timing recorded at 20:41, 21:41 and 00:11 UTC. One more exchange with the satellite was recorded between those times, at 22:41:22 UTC. It was not included in the catalogue runner’s fitting objective or selection criteria. This page reports what happens when each flight’s prediction for that moment is set beside the recorded value. The project calls this a withheld-measurement consistency check with disclosed prior exposure, and says below what that name does and does not claim.

These are counts of related model flights, not probabilities, and nothing here is a location. The 266 flights share their starting arrivals, their fitted measurements and one model. Flights of one incoming speed and cruise level have nearly the same prediction, so they tend to share one outcome and are not independent tests. A model state in the air at 22:41 is a conditional reconstruction; this check does not establish the aircraft’s actual position or where the flight ended.

Result 18 November 2026

At 22:41:22 UTC the ground station recorded a burst timing offset of {{q_obs_timing}} µs and a burst frequency offset of {{q_obs_freq}} Hz. After timing calibration and accounting for the ground-station leg, the model relates a timing residual to the two-way aircraft–satellite signal path. One microsecond corresponds to about {{q_m_per_us}} m of aircraft–satellite slant-range difference. This is a range equivalent, not a displacement on the Earth’s surface. The criterion is the one that accepted these flights at the fitted times: the recorded timing minus the predicted timing must be within ±{{q_band}} µs, about {{q_band_km}} km of aircraft–satellite slant range. It was fixed in writing before any 22:41 prediction for these catalogue flights was formed.

The distances in metres and kilometres are the excess beyond the band expressed as distance between aircraft and satellite along the line joining them (half the speed of light times the timing excess, since the timing is of a two-way path). They are not errors of the aircraft’s position over the ground.

The edge of the band passes through the arrival-and-cruise groups containing both outcomes, and a few microseconds separate the two outcomes within them. The outcomes themselves are numerically firm: computed at half the integration step, every prediction moves by at most {{q_step_timing}} µs, and no outcome changes.

Outside the band does not mean shown to be impossible. The numerical firmness belongs to the arithmetic. Agreement between integration steps does not measure the model’s physical accuracy. This check does not propagate measurement uncertainty or uncertainty in the satellite state, calibration, weather fields and commanded flight history into probabilities for individual flights. A flight {{q_exc_min}} µs outside the band and one {{q_in_margin_min}} µs inside it receive different labels under the fixed rule; this check does not quantify their relative physical plausibility. What can be said is that {{q_out}} flights do not meet the declared criterion at 22:41 and {{q_meet}} do. The {{q_out}} outside the band are not shown by this check alone to be physically impossible, and the {{q_meet}} inside it are not confirmed. The log reports timing in steps of {{q_log_grid}} µs. Descriptive scatter estimates from selected received-unit records span {{q_log_sd_lo}} to {{q_log_sd_hi}} µs under the assumptions in the next section; they do not calibrate an in-flight error distribution.

Two further thresholds were named beforehand for description only: {{q_within29}} of {{q_all}} flights are within ±29 µs and all {{q_within129}} within ±129 µs. They do not alter the outcome at ±{{q_band}} µs. A smaller residual does not rank one flight above another.

What the ground-station log says about the scale of the measurement added 18 November 2026

After the comparison was complete, the full ground-station log for the aircraft ({{q_log_units}} signalling units, 7–8 March 2014) was set beside the project’s inputs and results. No flight was recomputed: this is arithmetic on recorded values and on the residuals above.

The inputs are the log’s. Of the {{q_log_events}} entries of the project’s event table, {{q_log_single}} are single received units, and each is in the log once, on the stated channel, with the same timing and frequency. The 22:41 value used above is the log’s own, with no correction. The other {{q_log_voice}} entries are the two voice calls at 18:39 and 23:14. Each is a frequency averaged over every unit of the call: {{q_log_v1_n}} units on {{q_log_v1_channels}} channels with a mean of {{q_log_v1_mean}} Hz, entered as {{q_log_v1_table}} Hz, and {{q_log_v2_n}} units with a mean of {{q_log_v2_mean}} Hz, entered as {{q_log_v2_table}} Hz. The values are reproduced; the table names one channel for the first call and gives each call a single time, where the units span about a minute. Neither call enters the timing criterion by which the 266 flights were accepted or the 22:41 check. The last unit the log holds from the aircraft is the one at {{q_log_last_rx}}; the {{q_log_after}} later units, to {{q_log_end}}, are transmissions from the ground station.

The log reports timing in steps of {{q_log_grid}} µs. Every one of its {{q_log_rbursts}} timing offsets on the channel type used for these exchanges is a whole multiple of {{q_log_grid}} µs, about {{q_log_grid_km}} km of distance between aircraft and satellite. A reported {{q_obs_timing}} µs is quantized at {{q_log_grid}} µs resolution. That reporting step does not bound the total measurement uncertainty or the true flight timing. The published BTO error characterization supporting the fixed criterion already includes measurement quantization, so the reporting step supplies no extra allowance.

Descriptive scatter estimates span {{q_log_sd_lo}} to {{q_log_sd_hi}} µs. The selected predeparture interval is {{q_log_gate_from}} to {{q_log_gate_to}} UTC at Kuala Lumpur, within the half hour used for the investigation’s timing calibration. Treating the aircraft as stationary then is an assumption; the log contains no position. Its {{q_log_gate_bursts}} received-unit records range from {{q_log_gate_lo}} to {{q_log_gate_hi}} µs and mix channels and receiving units different from the combination used for the flight measurement. The four estimates spanning {{q_log_sd_lo}} to {{q_log_sd_hi}} µs combine statistics of this ground sample with a close-pair estimate from the whole log. The reported spreads use the population-denominator convention, including after detrending; the pair-based estimates include overlapping pairs and require assumptions about motion and error correlation. These are descriptive scales, not an independently calibrated error distribution for an in-flight observation. If used only as divisors, they make the ±{{q_band}} µs half-width {{q_log_band_sd_lo}} to {{q_log_band_sd_hi}} times those scales. That ratio assigns neither a probability nor normal-distribution coverage.

The timing slope estimated across the neighbouring recorded exchanges is about half a microsecond per second. Applying that rough slope to the timestamp difference gives {{q_log_lag_us}} µs, more than the {{q_exc_min}} µs excess of the nearest flight beyond the band. What this changes. No outcome above is altered: the outcomes are the declared criterion applied as fixed beforehand. The {{q_out}} flights have total residual magnitudes equal to the {{q_band}} µs half-width plus their {{q_exc_min}} to {{q_exc_max}} µs excess. The excesses are smaller than the reporting step; that does not put the whole residual inside a reporting step or make it negligible relative to the descriptive scatter. The {{q_out}} remain outside the declared band. This check alone does not establish that their histories are physically impossible, and it does not confirm the histories inside the band. The scatter estimates mix a selected ground sample and close pairs from the wider log under stated assumptions; they are not a calibrated in-flight error model. The investigation’s own calibration over that half hour reports a largest error of 59 µs among seventeen measurements. A maximum error and a descriptive scatter are different summaries, and neither supplies an extra allowance to the fixed criterion. Widening the band now, with the result in view, would be a criterion chosen after the fact and was not done.

How the check was run

  1. The method was written and fixed first. The population (all 266 flights), the recorded event and its fields, the sign of the residual, the criterion and the wording of each possible outcome were set down before any 22:41 prediction for these catalogue flights was formed. The program was reviewed, and faults found in review were repaired, before it was allowed to run.
  2. A gate on eleven flights. One flight of each combination was flown again from its stored commands. Each reproduced its catalogue record exactly, and the routine that forms a prediction gave the catalogue’s own stored values at 21:41 and 00:11. Nothing was evaluated at 22:41. The gate was run twice, once for each of two revisions of the program: a fault in its record-keeping was repaired between them, and a gate qualifies only the program it ran.
  3. Predictions for all 266 flights. Every flight was flown again and reproduced its record. One prediction of timing and frequency at 22:41:22 was then formed at the catalogue’s integration step and one at half that step: {{q_evals}} evaluations in all, none repeated. The two steps agree to within {{q_step_timing}} µs and {{q_step_freq}} Hz. The predictions were fixed, with a hash, before the recorded value was selected.
  4. The comparison. Only then was the recorded value selected and subtracted. The comparison was made by the project’s independent reviewer (Codex, an AI model that also reviewed each earlier step), with the program fixed in step 1, and again with a second program written separately from the method in 50-digit decimal arithmetic. The two agree on all {{q_all}} outcomes. The first program, run again on the computing machine, gave the same files byte for byte.

The predicted timings span {{q_pred_spread}} µs across the 266 flights ({{q_pred_min}} to {{q_pred_max}} µs), less than the {{q_band_full}} µs width of the band. That spread was known from the predictions alone, before the comparison. Whether all, some or none meet the band also depends on where the recorded value places it. The spread across group means is {{q_pred_between}} µs.

What “withheld” means here, and the limits

  • Withheld from the fit, not unknown. The 22:41 timing entered no search, adjustment or selection that produced these flights, and the cruise run’s guard recorded no request for a prediction there. But the value is public and is shown elsewhere in this Atlas; earlier experiments of this project formed predictions at 22:41 and compared other flights with it; the people and models who chose this catalogue’s speeds, levels and calibration worked after those comparisons existed; and the flight model loads the whole recorded event table, 22:41 included, whenever it starts. The runs of this check that did so are logged. It is not a test against a measurement nobody had seen, and that the catalogue’s design was uninfluenced by earlier 22:41 results cannot be shown.
  • An interpolation along a fitted leg. Each flight already matches the timing at 21:41 and at 00:11. Its last commanded turn starts between 20:56 and 21:26; after it the flight holds its commanded Mach number and pressure level with no further commanded turn. Held Mach is not constant ground speed, since temperature and wind change along the leg, but a prediction partway along a leg that is matched at both ends is constrained by both. The check mainly tests whether the commanded speed and level, the weather fields and the satellite model carry a flight correctly across that leg. Agreement at 22:41 is weaker evidence than agreement at a time the flights were not built around.
  • One model, fixed inputs. One satellite ephemeris and calibration, one wind and temperature field, the stored commands. No uncertainty is attached to a prediction. The spread across flights is a spread across accepted histories, not an error bar. The sensitivity to the satellite inputs identified in the separate satellite audit remains open.
  • Exact reproduction is local. Every flight reproduced its record to the last digit on the machine and numerical libraries that built the catalogue. That is not a claim about another machine.
  • Conditional states. At 22:41:22 the 266 model flights are in the air between {{q_lat_range}} and {{q_lon_range}}. Those are states of model flights at one moment. They are not an estimate of the aircraft’s position and not a search area.
  • Nothing about the end of the flight. The check concerns the cruise. It does not test the final transmissions, the descent or an impact position.
