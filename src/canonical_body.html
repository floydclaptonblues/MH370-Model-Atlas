Canonical reference map observations only

Everything on this page is a measurement, a published figure, or a third-party reanalysis product. No output of this project’s own modelling appears here — no reverse-drift density, no candidate endpoints, no Average Day flights, no fitted rings. The list of what is deliberately excluded is at the foot of the page.

Two things this map shows that the rest of the atlas does not. The published 7th arc is drawn in full, both arms, from 43.9°S to 45.0°N — the canonical observation never excluded the northern arm. And the satellite is drawn where the published state table puts it, which is not a fixed point: it drifts between 0.53°N and 1.64°N over the eight hours that matter.

Ten rows carry a BTO. Nine are flagged for BTO use and five for BFO use in the nine-term score. The 19:41 BTO is deliberately not scored, because a start constrained to the first ring satisfies it by construction.

Why only one arc is drawn

Ten handshakes carry a canonical BTO, so in principle there are ten rings. Only the published 7th arc is drawn, and the reason is worth stating rather than leaving as an omission.

The published arc is a circle: measured against a centre of 0.5327°N, 64.3347°E it has an angular radius of 44.4701° with a standard deviation of 0.0093° across all 200 points — a spread of 4.1 km. So the ring geometry is recoverable from published data and is not an invention of this project.

Deriving the other nine rings needs one more thing: the constant that converts a BTO reading into a slant range, absorbing the ground-station leg and the instrument bias. Solving for that constant from the first ring and from the seventh gives two answers that differ by 573 µs, which is 86 km of range. A single constant does not reconcile them, so the remaining rings cannot be drawn to a standard this page is willing to call canonical.

What is missing, specifically. The published BTO bias constant and the ground-station position. The station-to-satellite leg appears twice in every BTO and changes as the satellite drifts, so it cannot be folded into a constant across eight hours. The project record separately flags the BFO bias calibration epoch as MISSING with a conflict on its locator; this is the BTO counterpart and it is equally unresolved. Supply either and the other nine rings become drawable here.

The debris record

32 items at 21 sites. The item descriptions, find dates and regions come from the find record. The coordinates do not — they are unverified place-name geocodes with a nominal 100 km radius, which is why each site is drawn as a circle rather than a point. One of them, Saint-André on Réunion, failed to verify against an external source.

There is no observed drift path. The find record gives a place and a date for each item and nothing in between. Every line that has ever been drawn from a find back toward an origin — including every line in this project — is a model output, which is why none appears on this page. The currents above and the finds here are the two ends; the middle is inference.
