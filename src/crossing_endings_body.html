Where the tested flights cross the 19:41 ring, and where they end

Two results from the flights computed so far. The first is where flights that turn south between 18:40 and 19:41 meet the satellite timing ring at 19:41. The second puts every flight that has passed all of the project's tests against the seabed that has been searched. Both are statements about model flights under stated assumptions. Neither is a location for the aircraft.

How to read this page. A shaded area or a band of points shows where tested model flights go. Its size says nothing about likelihood. "Searched" means sonar data exists there, not that wreckage would have been detected. Only the flight types the project has flown are represented; a flight type not yet flown could cross or end elsewhere.

The 19:41 crossing 102 starting flights complete

Each flight starts from one of the project's accepted aircraft states at 18:40:12 UTC, makes one turn toward the south, and flies to 19:41:03. For each starting flight, turn time and speed, the computer adjusts the course after the turn until the flight's distance from the satellite at 19:41:03 matches the measured timing to within one microsecond (about 150 m of range). A flight is then accepted as an arrival if its Doppler frequency at that moment is also within 7 Hz of the measurement.

Result. The run returned 765 accepted arrivals, 700 of them distinct. Every one meets the 19:41 ring between 2.10°S and 1.27°N, at longitudes from 93.49°E to 93.75°E. The ring runs almost north–south there, so the narrow range of longitude follows from requiring the flights to be on the ring; the informative quantity is the stretch of ring they occupy, about 375 km.

What the turn itself looks like. All three turn times produce accepted arrivals. A turn at 18:40:12 needs a course of 187–198° afterwards; a turn at 19:00, 180–226°; a turn at 19:20, 165–227°. The satellite measurements at 19:41 therefore constrain where the turn delivers the aircraft far more tightly than they constrain when the turn was made or how sharp it was.

Conditions. The result holds for the reconstruction of the flight up to 18:40 that these starting states come from, and for the speeds, levels and turn times tested. A different reconstruction before 18:40 has not been tested. Eight further turn times and the remaining 707 starting flights have not been run. All 13 families are represented; 700 arrivals from related starting flights are not 700 independent confirmations.

Where the tested flights end 221 flights, through the final transmissions

A flight is counted here only if it has passed every test the project applies, in order: an accepted arrival at 19:41:03; satellite timing within 43 microseconds at 20:41:05, 21:41:27 and 00:11:00; support by the aircraft performance model throughout; fuel exhaustion at a time from which the end of the flight can be computed; and at least one computed end-of-flight response that agrees with the two final transmissions at 00:19:29 and 00:19:37. The 22:41 measurements are withheld from all of these tests.

At 00:19:37 every one of these states is within 21 km of arc 7, between 3.8 and 11.1 km above the surface, and descending at 69 to 121 m/s (roughly 14,000 to 24,000 ft/min) in a banked turn. No tested flight ends between about 34.1°S and 35.5°S. Flights do pass through that stretch: some run out of fuel there and reach the final transmissions further south. It is a gap in the flights flown, not evidence that no flight ends there.

Passing the final-transmission test did not separate the northern flights from the southern ones: all 130 flights of the second group have at least one compatible response.

Against searched seabed coverage partly unverified

The rules for this comparison were written down before any area was computed. Two layers of search coverage are used, and they are of different quality.

  • Documented. The Geoscience Australia deep-tow sonar footprint from the 2014–2017 search, 103,969 km². The project holds this geometry.
  • Reported, not verified. Ocean Infinity's 2018 search, described by an independent analyst as covering the arc to 25°S at widths of 22 nautical miles or more either side. The project holds no map of it. It is modelled here as that band between 25°S and 36°S.

Not included: Ocean Infinity's 2025–26 search (no map held), and gaps, poor-quality lines and terrain shadows inside either layer (not located).

If the descent continued to the surface at the rate each state has at 00:19:37, the flights reach the sea within 7 to 33 km of their 00:19:37 position. The area they can reach is 43,300 km².

If the descent was arrested conditional

Every tested state is high enough at 00:19:37 to glide if the descent were arrested. The data do not show that it was: the states are steep banked descents, and recovering from one takes deliberate, correct control inputs. The calculation below shows what a recovery would make reachable, under three stated assumptions: a glide ratio of 17, still air, and a recovery from the 00:19:37 state.

For a glide that continues roughly the way the aircraft was moving, the area outside both coverage layers is about 111,600 km², nearly all of it beyond the arc (the side farther from the satellite). It forms two strips: about 61,000 km² between 29°S and 35°S, starting at the outer edge of the reported 2018 band, and about 50,600 km² between 35°S and 40°S, beyond the outer edge of the documented footprint. The northern strip is spread evenly along its length (about 10,400 to 13,900 km² in each degree of latitude from 30°S to 35°S); the southern strip lies mostly between 36°S and 39°S. Nothing in the records favours one part over another.

The conditional statement the records support. If the tested flights represent the real one, and if the searches would have detected the wreckage, then the aircraft did not continue its descent to the sea near the arc, and a recovered glide is the remaining possibility. Each "if" is an assumption that has not been tested. An empty search is equally consistent with wreckage that was not detected, with gaps in the reported 2018 coverage, and with a flight of a type not yet modelled. This page does not assert that the aircraft glided.

What this does not establish

  • A location. These are endings of model flights. No probability is attached to any area.
  • Completeness. Only flight types already flown appear: great-circle steering after 19:41, the speeds, levels and turns of the earlier runs, and their starting points. The 700 new 19:41 arrivals above have not yet been carried forward to arc 7; a fixed subset of 74 is being continued now.
  • Detection. Coverage means sonar data exists. It is not a statement that wreckage there would have been found.
  • The 2018 coverage. It is modelled from a published description, with an assumed southern limit at 36°S.
  • The glide. The glide ratio, still air and direction are assumptions. The 00:19:37 track is a moment in a turn, not a settled heading.
  • Drift. Debris drift is not used as a filter here. Earlier runs found drift timing unable to separate positions along this stretch of the arc; a separate preregistered drift test is in preparation.
