How it works Satellite measurements, the fuel loop and the feasible-set formulation

A feasible-set formulation

This formulation describes how an evaluated family of flight states can be screened against declared physical and observational constraints. The resulting set depends on the starting population, command family, model and thresholds. The browser tool illustrates part of the fuel calculation; it does not evaluate the complete set below.

1

The admissible set — which flights survive

A = { (x0, u) ∈ X0 × U  :  dx/dt = f(x, u, w, θ), |ri| / σi ≤ κ  ∀ i ∈ I, β(x,t) ≥ 0, mf(t) ≥ 0 }
2

The reachable set — where powered flight ends

R = { π(x(τ))  :  (x0, u) ∈ A }, τ = inf{ t : mf(t) = 0 }
3

The determination map — three outcomes, not two

D(x0, u) = ⊥ if tdom < min(τ, tc)0 if τ ≤ tc and τ ≤ tdom1 if tc < τ and tc ≤ tdom

The data boundary is checked first. Fuel exhaustion exactly at the checkpoint is classified as power lost by that checkpoint.

4

The held-out test — computed, never used above

What the satellite actually measured

Satellite timing and frequency measurements constrain a flight in different ways. Timing supplies range information; frequency supplies information about relative motion and the radio system. Neither measurement by itself gives the aircraft's position.

BTO · burst timing offset

How long the signal took

The timing offset relates to the signal's round trip. After accounting for fixed delays and the ground-station leg, the timing model constrains aircraft–satellite slant range. A change in round-trip time corresponds to a range change of cΔt/2. Intersecting a range constraint with the assumed aircraft altitude produces an arc of possible positions.

Scale quoted in source example21.94 µs
Equivalent range scale, cΔt/2±3.3 km

This illustrates the timing-to-range conversion, not a universal error band for every message type. See Methods for the Atlas measurement conventions.

BFO · burst frequency offset

How the signal was shifted

Motion stretches or compresses a radio frequency, the same effect that drops the pitch of a passing siren. The measured shift combines the aircraft's own velocity, its rate of climb or descent, the satellite's drift in its orbit, and the correction the aircraft's own terminal was applying at the time. Unwind the known parts and what remains constrains the aircraft's direction and speed along the arc.

What it constrainsrelative motion
What it does not give alonea unique position

Different combinations of speed and heading can produce the same frequency shift. The ambiguity is real and is not wished away.

How a flight state becomes a predicted measurement

  1. A flight model produces a state at each epochPosition, altitude, velocity and vertical rate follow from the model's starting state, commands, forcing and dynamics.
  2. From that state the model predicts what the satellite would have measuredRange gives a predicted timing offset. The velocity vector, the satellite's own motion and the terminal's compensation give a predicted frequency offset.
  3. Prediction minus observation is the residualDividing by the assumed uncertainty scale makes the mismatch easier to compare. Acceptance or exclusion requires the experiment's declared measurement model and screen.
  4. Keep fitting and independent checks distinguishableAn observation reserved for checking must stay out of fitting and selection. This browser example performs neither a satellite fit nor a candidate selection.

One measurement is deliberately withheld

The example describes reserving the 22:41 BTO from fitting and selection, then comparing the completed flight's prediction against it. This does not mean the BFO at the same epoch is also withheld.

The residual provides an out-of-sample diagnostic. Interpreting a miss requires the declared uncertainty model and acceptance rule; a mismatch alone is not a calibrated rejection of the whole method. The current treatment is documented in Methods.

An open question, stated rather than buried

The supplied demonstration reports two timing-bias conventions forty microseconds apart. That difference corresponds to about six kilometres of slant range through cΔt/2.

A bias convention and any correction for a final log-on message must follow the applicable source and message model. This demonstration does not resolve either question; use the current Canonical data and Methods records.

This is why fuel and aerodynamics matter so much. The satellite data alone leaves a long arc and a family of headings that fit it. What narrows that down is the question of which of those flights the aircraft could physically have flown on the fuel it was carrying.

The aircraft is an object, not a dot

In this example, fuel burn depends on drag, drag depends on the lift needed to support the current mass, and mass decreases as fuel is burned. The browser recomputes that loop each minute while holding the atmosphere and commanded speed fixed.

  1. Mass falls as fuel is burnedEach step subtracts the burned fuel from both the inventory and total mass.
  2. Less weight needs less lift in steady level flightAt fixed airspeed and density, the required lift coefficient falls as the aircraft lightens.
  3. The induced part of drag fallsThe simple drag polar responds to the lower lift coefficient while its parasite term stays fixed.
  4. Lower drag means lower flowWhich feeds back into the mass, and the loop closes. Altitude and speed set the gain.

The fuel calculation, written out

Total massm = mairframe + mfuel(t)Fuel burned is mass gone. Only the first term is fixed.
Dynamic pressureq = ½ ρ V²Depends on air density and air-relative speed. Both are held fixed in this browser example.
Lift coefficientCL = m g / (q S)Falls as the aircraft lightens. This is the link from fuel burned to aerodynamics.

S is wing area, ρ air density, g gravity, c the specific fuel consumption, and kflow a flow multiplier. The demonstration retains the supplied pair 1.000000 and 1.024738, a 2.47 % difference.

Air density

At a given airspeed, lower density changes both dynamic pressure and the lift coefficient required to support the aircraft.

Speed

Fixed Mach

The browser example holds Mach and sound speed fixed. Its simple drag polar does not model transonic drag rise.

Declared limit

Buffet margin

A heavy aircraft high up has little margin left. Flights that cross the frozen performance model's declared envelope are recorded as unsupported rather than carried forward.

The wind is not a detail

The wing responds to air-relative motion. The path over the ground follows the vector sum of air-relative velocity and wind. Keeping those two velocities distinct is essential when comparing a flight with satellite observations.

Where wind enters the physics

Airspeed and groundspeed

At unchanged air-relative speed and density, a headwind or tailwind does not by itself change dynamic pressure or drag. It changes ground velocity and therefore the distance covered in a given time. Changes in the commanded flight, route or atmospheric state can also change the fuel calculation.

Where the wind comes from

ERA5 reanalysis

The supplied method description uses ERA5 atmospheric reanalysis for 7–8 March 2014, sampled on a regular 0.25° latitude–longitude grid of 721 × 1440 points and pressure levels spanning cruise altitudes.

A reanalysis combines observations with an atmospheric model to reconstruct past conditions. Its fields are model estimates, not direct measurements at the aircraft. The browser demonstration does not load them.

A full flight model must interpolate atmospheric fields along the trajectory and respect their available extent. The supplied historical description records weather-domain exits as undetermined when they occur before the question is settled.

A weather-data boundary limits the supported calculation; it is not a physical boundary for the aircraft.

How the supplied population is counted

The historical snapshot reports 1,565 incoming histories. Of these, 1,523 admit all six schedules: 1,523 × 6 = 9,138 simulations. The remaining 42 histories are retained as out-of-bounds records, bringing the reported register to 9,180 entries.

The source describes six schedules: a slow and fast edge, three intermediate settings and one holding the arrival condition steady. Their spread measures sensitivity within that selected family, not the full uncertainty over every possible flight.

The model is allowed to say it does not know

For a question about power at a checkpoint, a supported calculation can show power available, show fuel exhaustion, or end before either is established. The unresolved category must remain distinguishable from a demonstrated exclusion.
