Canonical data sources and derived reference layers

The canonical collection

This section is the data front door of the atlas: a source-linked collection of the case’s observations, published datasets and derived reference layers. Original observations and supplied datasets live on this page and on Satcom (the 5,029-row Inmarsat burst log and the canonical event table) and Reference map (every dataset's source, format and where to obtain it). Normalised tables, conventions and calculated outputs are kept separate and labelled as such on their own pages. The baseline ocean forcing for the drift chapter is the Copernicus Marine GLORYS12V1 global reanalysis, 2014–2018 — the same product named on the drift pages — with NCEP/NCAR 10 m winds. Individual experiments state their own forcing and temporal resolution and those statements govern: the forward-transport runs use monthly-mean winds, and the four-year chapter includes comparisons against other named ocean products. Monthly current and wind fields as used by the site are in fields/.

The page distinguishes observations and published tables from reanalysis products and project-derived display geometry. The map includes ten derived BTO rings, place-name geocodes for debris and approximate search boxes; these are not raw measurements. Candidate flight states, reverse-drift density and fitted flight paths are excluded. Each layer’s provenance and limitations govern its interpretation.

Map extent and satellite motion. The published 7th arc is drawn in full, both arms, from 43.9°S to 45.0°N — the canonical observation never excluded the northern arm. And the satellite is drawn where the published state table puts it, which is not a fixed point: it drifts between 0.53°N and 1.64°N over the eight hours that matter.

The −4,600 µs correction is applied on channel, and only there

The project corrects the raw BTO by −4,600 µs on exactly two events, and both are the R600 log-on requests — 18:25:27 on IOR-R600-0-36E1 and 00:19:29 on IOR-R600-0-36F8. Every scored handshake is IOR-R1200-0-36ED and carries correction zero. The correction is applied where the channel type differs and nowhere else, which is internally consistent. Whether −4,600 is itself the right magnitude is a calibration question, not an observation question.

The telephony averages check out

Two events are voice-channel bursts with no BTO, recorded in the project as averages. The log’s appendices list both in full. The 23:14 event is recorded as the mean of 29 BFO values spanning 216–222 Hz; Appendix 2 lists 216, 217, 218, 219, 220 and 222, consistent with the stated span and the recorded mean of 217. The 18:39 event is recorded as 51 values spanning 86–90 Hz; Appendix 1 shows 86–90.

What this settles, and what it does not. It settles that the measurement layer is faithful to the source: no transcription error, no mis-assigned channel, no timestamp drift. It does not validate the corrections, the uncertainties, or any model built on these numbers — those are separate questions, and some remain open. In particular the held-out 22:41 BTO residual is not a data-entry error, which is one of several explanations this check removes.

All ten BTO rings, derived from canonical data corrected 8 Oct

Ten handshakes carry a canonical BTO, so there are ten rings. All ten are drawn. The construction includes the time-varying ground-station–satellite leg; the earlier omission and its correction are documented below.

Correction history · timing-ring construction

An earlier calculation omitted the time-varying ground-station–satellite leg when comparing the first and seventh rings. The resulting constants differed by 573 µs, equivalent to 86 km of range. The leg is computable from the published satellite states; the discrepancy did not show that timing rings were underivable.

Computing it closes the gap. The satellite’s range to the ground station falls by 82.3 km between 19:41 and 00:19 as it drifts south, which moves the BTO term by −549 µs. The disagreement was −573 µs. The moving station leg accounts for 95.8% of it, leaving 24 µs — 3.6 km, comparable to the published arc’s own scatter about a circle.

The method, and what it rests on

Each ring is solved from the canonical BTO, the published satellite state at that moment, and the station leg, with a single bias calibrated on the published 7th arc alone. Every other ring, including the first, is then a free prediction. Aircraft altitude is taken as 40,000 ft, the published arc’s own reference; altitude sensitivity is about 5.5 µs per km, so even a 2 km error moves a ring by under 2 km.

Why this matters beyond the picture. The arc-1 ring is the spine of the arc-1 sweep: every candidate start in that work is placed on it. And the 19:41 BTO is deliberately not scored, on the stated grounds that a start constrained to the ring satisfies it by construction. That reasoning assumed the ring was right, and the one measurement that could have caught an error in it was the one switched off. It is now checked against published data instead of assumed, and it holds to 6.4 km. Had it come back 130 km out — which is what a single constant implied — every start in the sweep would have been systematically displaced with nothing in the score able to detect it.
The one number here that is not from the archive. The ground station is taken as Perth at 31.80°S, 115.887°E, supplied from general knowledge, not from any project file. It is the only external input on this page and it is load-bearing for the rings. A station 2° further south would make this residual vanish, but that sensitivity does not establish the true station position or justify tuning it. An authoritative ground-station coordinate is required for a calibrated derivation. The rings are drawn as derived, not canonical, for exactly this reason.

The debris record

32 items at 21 sites. The item descriptions, find dates and regions come from the find record. The coordinates do not — they are unverified place-name geocodes with a nominal 100 km radius, which is why each site is drawn as a circle rather than a point. One of them, Saint-André on Réunion, failed to verify against an external source.

There is no observed drift path. The find record gives a place and a date for each item and nothing in between. Every line that has ever been drawn from a find back toward an origin — including every line in this project — is a model output, which is why none appears on this page. The currents above and the finds here are the two ends; the middle is inference.

Are the observations scored against their proper channel? audit, 29 Oct 2026, corrected same day

The signalling log labels every burst with a channel and a message type, and those labels matter: the ground station applies a different timing offset to a log-on burst than to a routine handshake, and the published uncertainty differs by burst type. This audit changes no value and moves no candidate.

The record is better organised than a first pass suggested

Every observation in the project’s event table carries a declared role and an explicit include-or-exclude flag, and those roles account for most of what looked at first like inconsistent treatment. Seven distinct roles are in use, among them one for bursts excluded as restart transients, one for rounded-epoch copies kept only for diagnosis, one for native-precision scored observations, and one for terminal observations held as envelope sources rather than scored. Where two apparently similar bursts are weighted differently, it is because they were assigned different roles on purpose.

The two 18:28 bursts are a case in point. They appear twice: once as rounded-to-the-second copies marked diagnostic-only and excluded, and once at native millisecond precision marked as scored. That is a deliberate distinction between a reading convenience and the measurement actually used — not a duplication.

And the −4,600 µs log-on offset is applied to both log-on bursts, 17,120→12,520 µs and 23,000→18,400 µs, both confirmed against the ground-station log.

What does remain open, and it is narrower

Three questions about the uncertainty values themselves, not about which bursts are used.

• The scored log-on burst is on a different channel from the hourly handshakes, yet carries the handshake-derived timing uncertainty. The published figure for its channel is roughly three times larger.

• The scored telephony burst carries the frequency uncertainty derived from the signalling bursts. Whether that transfers to a telephony channel is not recorded anywhere.

• Two different message types share one timing uncertainty within the same scored role — an access request from early in the flight and the hourly handshakes. The published distinction between routine and anomalous bursts is itself a message-type distinction, and this project uses a single number for both.

A fourth, smaller point: the scored copies of the 18:28 pair leave the channel and message-type fields blank. The labels are recoverable from their diagnostic twins, so nothing is being weighted blindly, but the scored rows should carry them.

Correction history · observation-role audit

An earlier card incorrectly classified three declared role assignments as defects: restart-transient exclusions, rounded diagnostic copies and terminal envelope-source observations. Those allegations are withdrawn. The uncertainty questions above remain distinct from observation transcription and role assignment.

Signal path as drawn: each antenna panel connects to its beam steering unit, then to its diplexer / low-noise amplifier. The two receive outputs join in the combiner and go to the satellite data unit’s receiver. The data unit’s transmit output goes through the high-power relay, which selects the port or the starboard side.

Not established. The image carries no manufacturer, product name or part number, and nothing on it ties it to 9M-MRO. A port-and-starboard pair of flat panels is the general form of a side-mounted high-gain antenna, and the Inmarsat approval list names one such Boeing-line antenna, the Ball Aerospace “Airlink” high-gain antenna system. That resemblance is an observation, not an identification. If the source brochure can be named, this card will be corrected to cite it.
